# Shipping, updating and supporting Alpha at client firms: prior art (16 October 2026)

Why: Kenil wants to ship Alpha to a few client firms (1–20 people each, each on their own
install) pre-set with generic connectors, standard agents and client-specific workflow kits,
with no data in them, and then fix and update the app, the connectors and the kits for all of a
client's users without reaching their data or undoing what each person built or changed. The
proposal this informs is `../shipping-and-kits-proposal.md`; its eleven questions are what the
research was framed around. Six research passes ran on 16 Oct 2026 (packaged apps' upgrade
boundaries; integration platforms and connector versioning; agent skills and site know-how;
macOS distribution and update channels; local data safety and support without data access;
model and mailbox access), and this is their synthesis. Every claim carries its URL inline;
inferences and vendor claims are marked as such in the text; where sources disagree or a gap
remains, it is said in place. What the research changed in the proposal is recorded in the
proposal itself, not here.

---

## Separate Ownership, Sign Everything, Delete Nothing

Every proven platform that ships vendor-maintained things into customer environments draws the same boundary: it records a baseline for each shipped thing, gives the customer's changes a separate home, and on upgrade never deletes a thing the customer touched — Salesforce deprecates rather than deletes, ServiceNow skips the record and lists it for review, Power Platform leaves the customer's layer on top, dpkg and RPM keep the local file and write a side file, Shopify carries theme-editor settings forward and drops only conflicting code edits with a notice. The line between a silent fix and a change that asks is drawn by the customer's required action, not by code size: Airbyte, Fivetran, Stripe, Tray and Microsoft Teams apply changes automatically when existing inputs, credentials, schemas and outputs keep working, and ask — with a dated window and a stated end-state — when new input or new scopes are needed, while connections and credentials live in a per-customer record that the shipped artifact never contains. For connectors, **no agent-skill ecosystem signs code as of October 2026**: Claude Code pins commits and SHA-256 digests and leaves auto-update off for third-party marketplaces, ClawHub learned the cost of review-free publishing in the ClawHavoc campaign (January–February 2026), and site-automation vendors (Waalaxy, Dux-Soup, Phantombuster) moved site know-how into their clouds precisely so fixes ship without a client update; every self-healing framework surveyed keeps repairs per-customer or as drafts a human approves, and none shares repairs across customers. On macOS a Developer ID certificate plus notarization is the practical floor because macOS 15 removed the Control-click › Open bypass (Apple notice, 6 August 2024) and TCC grants are keyed to the code-signing designated requirement (TN3127); Sparkle 2 is the reference updater, and a signed index with an expiry and a monotonic serial plus per-artifact Ed25519 signatures is the minimum channel design. Local data survives upgrades in products that put a version number in the file, copy it before migrating, refuse to open newer data, check integrity, and only ever add to the schema; the documented disasters (Windows 10 1809, Lightroom mobile 5.4.0, iOS 17.5, Catalina Mail, Audacity 3.0) all rewrote user data in place with no copy and no post-migration check. Support without data access is an established norm — diagnostics reports the person owns and reviews (1Password, Signal, Firefox, VS Code) and customer-switched, time-boxed, logged access (Notion 28 days, Atlassian until the ticket closes). On compliance, Anthropic's live terms require each end user to authenticate with their own API key or their own subscription through the *unmodified* Claude Code binary and forbid a vendor paying for or intermediating usage "unless we've mutually agreed otherwise", with no published approval route beyond contact-sales; Outlook is reachable through delegated Graph scopes that need no admin consent, except that an unverified multitenant app triggers step-up admin consent; Gmail read scopes are restricted and need an annual CASA lab assessment unless the OAuth client is the firm's own Internal project; LinkedIn offers no messaging or connections API, its User Agreement (effective 3 November 2025) bans browser add-ons that scrape or automate, and the lowest-risk posture is per-person, human-paced, person-initiated use — risk reduced, not removed. Reading outlook.office.com or Gmail inside the person's own signed-in browser has no published safe harbour from either vendor.

### Proven platforms record a baseline, house customer changes separately, and deprecate instead of delete

Salesforce fixes the boundary per component *property* in platform rules it calls manageability rules, and the rule set is the clearest statement of the three-way split every other platform approximates. "Only Package Developer Can Edit: The package developer can edit specific component properties. These properties are locked in the subscriber's org. During package upgrade, the changes made by the package developer are applied in the subscriber org." "Both Subscriber and Package Developer Can Edit: Both the subscriber and package developer can edit these component properties, but developer changes are only applied to new subscriber installs. This approach prevents a package upgrade from overwriting changes made by the subscriber." "Neither Subscriber or Package Developer Can Edit: After a package is promoted and released, these component properties are locked" — API names, for instance ([Editable Properties After Package Promotion or Installation](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg1-dev/guide/mdc-editable-property-definitions.html)). The price of the middle class is that **vendor improvements to layouts and help text never reach existing installs**, only new ones (inferred from the rule). The companion rules add that a component the subscriber deleted is not reinstalled on upgrade, and that removing a released component "marks the component as deprecated, and doesn't hard delete the component from the subscriber org"; the feature itself must be requested through a Partner Support case ([Components Available in First-Generation Managed Packages](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg1-dev/guide/first-gen-packageable-components.html)). The second-generation page lists exactly which types are hard-deleted on upgrade — Apex classes and triggers (excluding global), Visualforce components, platform event channels, custom-metadata records invisible to the subscriber — and says everything else (objects, fields, flows, layouts, permission sets, reports, dashboards, Lightning components, named credentials, workflows) is "Marked as deprecated", logged in the Setup Audit Trail, deletable by the subscriber admin, and removed only on uninstall ([Remove Metadata Components from Second-Generation Managed Packages](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg2-dev/guide/sfdx-dev-dev2gp-remove-md-components.html)). The stated reason is that "This approach to component removal ensures that package upgrades don't disrupt a subscriber's org", and the same page documents the long-tail failure: a `project__c` deprecated in version 2.0 and never deleted by the subscriber makes the 5.0 upgrade fail years later when the vendor reuses the name ([What to Consider Before Removing Metadata Components](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg2-dev/guide/sfdx-dev-dev2gp-md-removal-considerations.html)). In first-generation packages a deleted component's name "remains within Salesforce, and you can't create a new component that uses the deleted component's name" ([Delete Components from First-Generation Managed Packages](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg1-dev/guide/viewing-deleted-components.html)).

Salesforce also shows how a vendor fixes a bug fleet-wide without consent, and how carefully it fences that power. Push upgrades let a vendor "upgrade second-generation managed packages installed in subscriber orgs, without asking customers to install the upgrade themselves", are gated on passing the AgentExchange security review and a Partner Support case, and obey "The same manageability rules" ([Push a Package Upgrade for 2GP](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg2-dev/guide/push-upgrade-intro-2gp.html)). Salesforce's own framing: "When you push an upgrade, you're making changes to a subscriber's org without explicit consent"; "Don't make visible changes to a package in a patch. This is because other than a change in the package version number, subscribers aren't notified of push upgrades"; enhancements to features users already have may be auto-assigned by a post-install script, but "New features you're introducing — Don't use a post install Apex script to auto-assign components" ([Push Upgrades (1GP)](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg1-dev/guide/packaging-push-about.html)). Its best-practice page is blunt: "Pushing an upgrade without proper planning and preparation can result in significant customer satisfaction issues"; "if you have 1,000 customers, push upgrades to 50 or 100 customers at a time, at least the first few times"; push to sandboxes first and "Give them a week or more to test"; "you can impact customer data, not just metadata" ([Push Upgrade Best Practices](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg1-dev/guide/push-upgrade-best-practices.html)). Scheduling runs through `PackagePushRequest`/`PackagePushJob` objects, beta versions are ineligible, and the suggested sequence is own orgs → a few customers' sandboxes → the wider base "based on your agreements with each customer" ([Scheduling Push Upgrades](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg1-dev/guide/push-upgrade-scheduling.html)); the subscriber "take[s] no action", sees only "a higher version number on the Package Detail page", and "the developer who started the push handles any upgrades that fail" ([Push Package Upgrades to Subscribers](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg1-dev/guide/push-upgrade-intro.html), search summary). Consent is handled by contract and process rather than by the platform asking each org, and a vendor can hand customers a block switch (`PushUpgradeCustomization`) ([Enable a Package Subscriber to Restrict Push Upgrades](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg2-dev/guide/push-upgrade-customized.html), search summary). The fix-versus-feature split is enforced structurally: a patch version (`major.minor.patch.build` with a non-zero patch) "can't: Add package components. Delete existing package components… Add features, settings, package dependencies, or web services", inherits the upgrade path of its base version, and needs Partner Support enablement plus security review ([Patch Versions for 2GP](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg2-dev/guide/sfdx-dev-dev2gp-create-patch-version.html)). One Apex `InstallHandler.onInstall` script runs on every install and upgrade and "If the script fails, the install or upgrade is aborted" ([Customize 2GP Installs and Uninstalls Using Scripts](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.pkg2_dev.meta/pkg2_dev/sfdx_dev_dev2gp_customize_installs.htm); [InstallHandler Interface](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.apexref.meta/apexref/apex_install_handler.htm), search summaries). No public incident of a push upgrade damaging subscriber orgs was found in this research; the risk is documented only by Salesforce's warnings and by the existence of the block feature.

ServiceNow's unit of ownership is the record and its "customized" bit is set by history. "The system skips upgrading records you have customized and provides you with a list of these skipped records" ([Upgrade Monitor, Zurich, updated 31 Jul 2025](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/7LhbZn3yz3Q58qhjZoU9iQ/nm~vHI0jLuB_rB~1zIqm~w)); when an object is customized a row lands in Customer Updates (`sys_update_xml`) and "The upgrade process skips changes to objects that have entries in the table"; each skipped record is then retained, merged through a Diff/Merge tool, or reverted with "Revert to Base System" (reversible via "Reapply Changes"), and only "Not Reviewed" records stay on the to-do list ([Resolve a skipped update](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/7LhbZn3yz3Q58qhjZoU9iQ/64DtDeR7Htz9ga6Ep7uOEA); [Revert a customization](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/SbrhqYG7UKQCk7uYwvgVBA/mdrbG5mNRMXryJfhGu2hQw), search summary). Fields marked `update_exempt` (the Active field by default) are never overwritten ([Vancouver docs](https://docs.servicenow.com/bundle/vancouver-platform-administration/page/customer-support/task/revert-customization.html), search summary), an Upgrade Preview predicts the skip list ([Preview predicted changes](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/UrSRFFKWBbfQBgoRlt~ltw/l4eENUmM2VdpkXaE1kLXTw), search summary), and the review burden is real: because "An upgrade will skip every record that was ever customized/changed" — even one whose value was later restored, so "you should revert instead of just changing the value back" — one admin manually reviewed **about 430 skipped updates in roughly six hours** ([ServiceNow Community, 2016](https://www.servicenow.com/community/servicenow-ai-platform-forum/skipped-updates-appear-to-have-no-conflicts/m-p/1185559)). Vendor deletions are elective: they ship in an `author_elective_update` folder "packaged but not applied by default", with `com.glide.apps.include_my_deletes` defaulting to false; Store-installed scoped apps generate "Skipped records… customization changes applied", and a separate "Customization" application scope for customer changes produces "No skipped records… unless the customization updates the base application record" ([Skipped records during application installation, Zurich](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/zurich/platform-administration/generation-skip-records-app-installs.html)). Protection policies let a vendor lock files (Read-only, or Protected = encrypted on Store installs, changeable only by a ServiceNow employee), but a file the customer already customized "maintains the default protection policy of Write" even if the vendor later marks it Read-only, inheriting the new policy only when reverted to baseline ([Application file protection policy, Yokohama](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/SmYvGPN0OygSQs53fsexIQ/pqB~7F5LOiO2Uvfy_JpYUQ); [Community thread on Store semantics](https://www.servicenow.com/community/app-engine-forum/can-customers-see-scripts-of-a-protected-scoped-app-published-on/m-p/3380887)). Scoping gives each app a namespace that "uniquely identifies them and their associated artifacts" and restricts cross-app writes ([Application scope, Zurich](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/zurich/application-development/c_ApplicationScope.html)); third-party vendors such as HappySignals tell customers to process skipped records after *their* updates too ([HappySignals](https://support.happysignals.com/handling-skipped-records-during-happysignals-application-updates)). A 2025 practitioner post adds the subtler failure that "Technically the upgrade fixed a bug. Practically, it broke your users' workflows that had evolved around that bug" ([Echelon AI, 17 Dec 2025, vendor blog](https://echelonai.com/blog/surviving-servicenow-upgrades-5-surprises-that-catch-teams-off-guard)); review statuses and prioritisation by file type are described in [Review skipped records (Yokohama)](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/yokohama/platform-administration/upgrade-center/uc-access-rl.html) and platform-upgrade re-application in [xMatters support](https://support.xmatters.com/hc/en-us/articles/24468349577371-Upgrading-the-xMatters-app-for-ServiceNow-Washington-DC) (search summaries).

Microsoft's Dataverse is the closest production analogue to "owner-tagged things with the customer's layer on top". Managed solutions import as stacked managed layers; "All unmanaged solutions share a single unmanaged layer" that sits above them, "Last one wins" or merge logic resolves conflicts, and uninstalling a managed solution lets the one below take effect ([Solution layers and merge behavior, 30 Jan 2025](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/alm/solution-layers-alm)). Customer edits persist across vendor upgrades and mask them: "Unmanaged customizations reside at the top layer for a component and subsequently define the runtime behavior", the maker UI shows "Unmanaged layer" in the Solution column with an "Updated" tab of changed properties, and "Remove active customizations" deletes that layer irreversibly ([Solution layers (maker), 9 Jan 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/maker/data-platform/solution-layers)). A practitioner describes the symptom — a production edit leaves an unmanaged layer that "will remain in place. leaving you with the feeling that the solution is failing to deploy" — and the fix, turning off the managed property "Allow customizations" ([SharePains, 2022/2024](https://sharepains.com/2022/01/19/disable-editing-app-flow-power-platform/)). Managed properties "are intended to protect your solution from modifications that might cause it to break", can be changed only by the original publisher's update, and are viewable but not editable by importers ([View and edit managed properties, 4 Feb 2025](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/alm/managed-properties-alm)). **Deletion is opt-in per import action**: "Upgrade… Any components associated to the previous solution version that aren't in the newer solution version will be deleted"; "Update… Components that aren't in the newer solution won't be deleted"; and "Overwrite Customizations (not recommended)… overwrites or removes any unmanaged customizations" ([Update a solution, 20 May 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/maker/data-platform/update-solutions)); a patch "can't delete any components or assets from the parent solution" and "Using patches isn't recommended" ([Create and update custom solutions for ALM](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/alm/update-solutions-alm)). Pipelines "default import behavior is Upgrade without Overwrite customizations", cannot deploy unmanaged solutions, prevent "any tampering or modification to the exported solution artifact", and from October 2026 warn admins deploying to unmanaged targets with 30 days before blocking ([Overview of pipelines, 29 Sep 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/alm/pipelines); [Solution concepts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/alm/solution-concepts-alm)). Credentials and per-environment values stay outside the package by design: a connection reference is "a solution component that contains a reference to a connection", so flows bind to the reference and "a connection is provided for all the connection references" at import ([Use a connection reference, 9 Jan 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/maker/data-platform/create-connection-reference)); an environment variable separates a managed definition from an unmanaged value so that "When the application publisher publishes updates to the application, the value set by the customer isn't overwritten", with "Should I include the value in my solution? No" and secrets held in Azure Key Vault ([Use environment variables, 9 Jan 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/maker/data-platform/environmentvariables)). Microsoft documents no cross-tenant push; pipelines "can't deploy to a different tenant".

Shopify is the clearest production demonstration that moving customer *settings and content* into data files is what makes automatic vendor updates safe — and that customer *code* edits remain the unsolved residue. Online Store 2.0, announced at Shopify Unite on 29 June 2021, moved merchant settings into `settings_data.json` and JSON templates that "store a list of sections to be rendered, and their associated settings", including merchant custom CSS ([Shopify Unite 2021](https://www.shopify.com/partners/blog/shopify-unite-2021); [Online Store 2.0](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/os20); [JSON templates](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/templates/json-templates)). The developer-side rule: "A theme is automatically updated only if all theme files, except `settings_data.json` and any JSON files in the `/templates` directory, are in their original state"; otherwise the update lands as an unpublished theme, "The parent theme is not modified", changing a setting ID or removing a section forces a manual update, "If you change an existing setting so that the merchant's value is no longer valid, then the merchant's value will be reset to the default", versions follow X.Y.Z, and a theme "must have a minimum of four weeks between updates" ([shopify.dev: Theme updates](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/store/success/updates)). The merchant-side rule: "Any customizations made to your theme using the theme editor are copied over and applied to the updated theme"; non-conflicting code edits are included, conflicting ones produce "Theme added: code edits could not be included" and must be copied by hand, the update arrives as a draft "prefixed with Updated copy of", Shopify's own automated updates "don't modify the look and feel of your theme or make any changes to your content or settings", and third-party themes from outside the Theme Store get no updates at all ([Shopify Help: Updating themes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/managing-themes/updating-themes)). The migration into 2.0 itself lost content — updater apps carry only global settings because "content added through the theme editor doesn't transfer to the new architecture" ([Pixel Union](https://support.pixelunion.net/hc/en-us/articles/9180687818899); [Out of the Sandbox](https://help.outofthesandbox.com/hc/en-us/articles/9186251139603-Should-I-use-Theme-Updater-app-to-migrate-my-vintage-theme); [Shopify Community on Liquid→JSON](https://community.shopify.com/t/can-i-preserve-section-settings-when-migrate-from-liquid-to-json-template/62484)) — and code-edit pain persists: a Dawn update removed a custom Liquid section (11 June 2024) and heavily customised Dawn 8.0 → 15.3 stores were told to rebuild ([Shopify Community, June 2024](https://community.shopify.com/t/updating-dawn-theme-removes-custom-liquid-from-template/324306/5); [Shopify Community, Dawn rebuild](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-to-update-custom-dawn-theme-without-losing-its-contents-sections-and-features/411512); [older thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/theme-customisations-dissapearing-on-update/64903)).

WordPress and Odoo solve the same problem with inheritance rather than data files. A WordPress child theme overrides parent files by name, both `functions.php` files load, and the Theme Handbook's benefit list includes "Allow parent themes to be updated without losing your modifications" — with the warning that if you edit the parent, "the next time your theme is updated, your function will disappear!" ([Theme Handbook: Child Themes](https://developer.wordpress.org/themes/advanced-topics/child-themes/)). Plugins have no child equivalent ("child themes don't override plugin files the way they do with the parent theme"; core edits "will be lost") ([wordpress.org support](https://wordpress.org/support/?p=13220764); [wordpress.org support](https://wordpress.org/support/?p=1403686), search summaries), so the documented remedy is hooks and options: after a StaffList user's hand-edited labels were wiped — "those changes will be reverted with every successive plugin update" — the author moved them into a settings panel so that "As these values are saved to your WP options, they will no longer be reverted" ([wordpress.org support, ~Jan 2019](https://wordpress.org/support/?p=11059540); same advice at [The Events Calendar](https://theeventscalendar.com/support/forums/topic/losing-script-customizations-on-updates/) and [Search & Filter](https://support.searchandfilter.com/forums/topic/lost-format/), search summaries). Odoo extends models in place with `_inherit` and views with `inherit_id` plus xpath "Instead of modifying existing views in place" ([Odoo 18 tutorial: Inheritance](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/developer/tutorials/server_framework_101/12_inheritance.html)); data records in a `<data noupdate="1">` block are "Only loaded when installing the module", everything else is reloaded on update ([Odoo 18: Data Files](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/developer/reference/backend/data.html); [2013 commit honouring noupdate](https://git.sysmocom.de/odoo/odoo/commit/a859a53e72931b09f88004803958ee23779c541f)). Odoo's upgrade service covers "all standard applications" and "all customizations created with the Studio app" but not modules "created in-house or by third parties, including Odoo partners" ([Odoo 17 Upgrade § SLA](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/17.0/administration/upgrade.html); [Odoo 18 Upgrade](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/administration/upgrade.html)); a documentation PR spells out the exclusion as "lines of code added to standard modules… code entered manually, and automated actions using Python code", wording not confirmed on a live page ([odoo/documentation PR #4365](https://github.com/odoo/documentation/pull/4365/files), search summary). Custom modules need upgrade scripts (`rename_field()`, `rename_model()`, `remove_module()`…), which cannot run on Odoo Online because Python custom modules are not allowed there ([Upgrade a customized database](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/developer/howtos/upgrade_custom_db.html)); Studio customisations export as a `customizations.zip` module whose dependencies Studio cannot infer ([Odoo 14: Export and Import Modules](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/user/14.0/studio/how_to/export_import.html), older). On Odoo.sh a failed upgrade is auto-reverted with a backup; on Odoo Online and on-premise "reverting to the previous version is not possible" once complete.

Home Assistant blueprints are the pure "shared template plus per-instance inputs" design and show what happens when the template has no per-instance ownership at all. An automation stores only `use_blueprint:` with a `path` and an `input` map, the blueprint holds `!input` placeholders ([Creating an automation blueprint](https://www.home-assistant.io/docs/blueprint/tutorial/)), and editing the blueprint means "The new changes will appear to your existing automations as well"; re-importing "overwrites the blueprint you currently have" and "If the re-imported blueprint is not compatible, it can break your automations"; the only escape is "Take control", which converts the automation to a standalone one and "You won't be able to convert this back into a blueprint" ([Using automation blueprints](https://www.home-assistant.io/docs/automation/using_blueprints/)). Deleting a blueprint file warns that "all the automations created with it will be deleted", and the feature request for a safe "Blueprint updater", opened 8 August 2023, was closed on 30 April 2025 as a duplicate of an unimplemented request ([HA Community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/blueprint-updater/600654)); authors' practice when inputs change is "create a new automation and manually enter your existing inputs" ([Community blueprint FAQ](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/bathroom-humidity-exhaust-fan/509992?page=15), search summary). The missing piece — a managed update path for the shared template that respects per-instance inputs — is exactly what a vendor-shipped kit needs.

The package managers state the canonical rule with a recorded baseline. Debian Policy §10.7 makes it a *must*: "local changes must be preserved during a package upgrade", configuration files "must be preserved when the package is removed, and only deleted when the package is purged", obsolete unchanged files "should be removed", and maintainer scripts "must not overwrite or otherwise mangle the user's configuration without asking" ([Debian Policy §10.7](https://www.debian.org/doc/debian-policy/ch-files.html)). dpkg's decision logic in `deferred_configure_conffile()`: identical → keep silently; unmodified locally and package changed → install new; modified locally and package unchanged → keep; **both changed → prompt, default keep**, with the strings "==> Modified locally since installation.", "==> Package distributor has shipped an updated version.", "Y or I : install the new package distributor's version", "N or O : keep the current locally installed version", "D : show the differences", "Z : start a shell" ([dpkg source, configure.c](https://salsa.debian.org/dpkg-team/dpkg/-/raw/main/src/main/configure.c)); the baseline is the md5sum of the shipped file in the Conffiles field, yours is saved as `.dpkg-old` when replaced and the new one as `.dpkg-dist` when kept ([Hertzog, 2010](https://raphaelhertzog.com/2010/09/21/debian-conffile-configuration-file-managed-by-dpkg/)); `--force-confold`/`--force-confnew`/`--force-confdef` choose without prompting, `--force-confmiss` "is dangerous, since it means not preserving a change (removing)", `--remove` keeps conffiles and `--purge` removes them ([dpkg(1)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man1/dpkg.1.html); unattended recipe in the [Debian Administrator's Handbook §5.2.3](https://debian-handbook.info/browse/stable/sect.package-meta-information.html)). The documented failure is a package that modifies its own conffile and thereby prompts users who never edited it, hanging non-interactive upgrades with "EOF on stdin at conffile prompt" ([piuparts template](https://piuparts.debian.org/templates/mail/prompting_due_to_modified_conffiles_which_were_not_modified_by_the_user.mail)). ucf extends the same behaviour to generated files with a hash database and `--three-way` diff3 merges ([ucf(1)](https://manpages.debian.org/unstable/ucf/ucf.1.en.html)). RPM has the same matrix with side files instead of prompts: "A locally modified %config(noreplace) is preserved as-is on package updates, but the new content from the package is saved with .rpmnew suffix", plain `%config` installs the vendor's and keeps yours as `.rpmsave`, and `.rpmorig` marks a pre-existing file ([rpm-spec(5)](https://rpm-software-management.github.io/rpm/man/rpm-spec.5)); Fedora's guideline is "use %config(noreplace) instead of plain %config unless your best, educated guess is that doing so will break things", with a spec comment whenever plain `%config` is used ([Fedora Packaging Guidelines](https://docs.fedoraproject.org/en-US/packaging-guidelines/#_configuration_files)); the empirical outcome matrix dates from RPM 4.2 ([Warbrick, c. 2003](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~jw35/docs/rpm_config.html)), and the one trap is that *dropping* a `%config(noreplace)` file from a package renames the local copy to `.rpmsave`, which "can silently unset local admin settings" ([HTCondor users, Nov 2014](https://www-auth.cs.wisc.edu/lists/htcondor-users/2014-November/msg00064.shtml), search summary).

Template updaters and Helm apply the baseline idea to whole projects with three-way merges. Copier "regenerates a fresh project from the current template version", diffs it against the current project, applies the new template "asking for confirmation", and "re-applies the previously obtained diff", with conflicts either inline (default) or as `.rej` files; it requires a clean git tree and a tagged template, warns "**Never** update `.copier-answers.yml` manually", excludes template files the project deleted, and offers `_skip_if_exists` (render once, recreate if missing), `_exclude` conditioned on `_copier_operation == 'update'` (render once, never update) and `_migrations` ([Copier: Updating](https://copier.readthedocs.io/en/latest/updating/); [Copier: Configuring](https://copier.readthedocs.io/en/stable/configuring/)). cruft records the template commit in `.cruft.json`, `cruft check` fails CI when out of date, `cruft update` renders old and new template and applies the diff with `git apply -3`, falling back to `--reject` and `.rej` files — and advances the recorded commit even then ([cruft docs](https://cruft.github.io/cruft/); [cruft update.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cruft/cruft/master/cruft/_commands/update.py)). Helm 3 (released 13 November 2019) "generates a patch using three inputs: the old manifest, the live state, and the new manifest", so manual live edits the chart does not touch survive — Helm 2's two-way patch silently removed them ([Changes since Helm 2](https://helm.sh/docs/faq/changes_since_helm2/); [Helm 3.0.0 released](https://helm.sh/blog/helm-3-released/)). The common mechanism across all six: "did the customer change it?" is derived from a recorded baseline (md5sum, file digest, template commit, last-applied manifest), never from a flag the customer sets, and side files are the universal "keep theirs, don't lose ours" fallback when a merge is impossible (inferred).

| Platform | How "customer changed this" is known | What the upgrade does to a customized thing | Is anything ever deleted? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salesforce managed packages | Per-property manageability class fixed by the platform | Developer-only properties overwritten; both-editable properties never upgraded on existing installs | Removed components deprecated, not deleted (except Apex/VF/event channels/invisible CMT records); names reserved forever |
| ServiceNow | Any history of change → `sys_update_xml` row | Vendor version skipped, listed under Skipped Changes to Review; retain/merge/revert | Vendor deletions shipped but not applied by default (`author_elective_update`) |
| Power Platform | Unmanaged layer on top of managed layers | Customer layer wins and masks the vendor change until removed | Only an "Upgrade" import deletes absent components; "Update" and patches never delete; dependencies block |
| Shopify themes | Any file other than `settings_data.json`/`templates/*.json` not in original state | Editor settings copied forward; code edits carried if non-conflicting, else dropped with a notice; arrives as a draft | Live theme untouched; invalid setting values reset to default |
| WordPress / Odoo | Child theme / inheritance module (no baseline) | Parent and plugin files overwritten; overrides survive | Plugin options kept until uninstall (`uninstall.php`) |
| Home Assistant blueprints | None (inputs only) | Template change applies to every automation at once | Deleting the blueprint deletes all its automations |
| dpkg / ucf / RPM | md5sum / digest of shipped file | Keep local if only local changed; prompt (dpkg) or `.rpmnew` (RPM) if both changed | Conffiles survive remove, die on purge; dropped `%config(noreplace)` becomes `.rpmsave` |
| Copier / cruft / Helm 3 | Recorded template commit / last manifest | Three-way merge; conflicts inline, `.rej`, or live state preserved | Files the project deleted are excluded from updates |

The documented failures cluster into five kinds: customer edits silently discarded because they lived in vendor files (WordPress, Shopify code edits, vintage theme migrations); vendor fixes silently *masked* by sticky customer layers (Power Platform's unmanaged layer, ServiceNow's skipped records); deprecation debris blocking a later upgrade (Salesforce's name collision); a shared template change breaking every instance (Home Assistant); and prompts in non-interactive contexts (dpkg). All of them trace back to one of two missing pieces — no recorded baseline, or no separate home for customer changes — and the platforms that have both (Dataverse layers, dpkg/RPM baselines, Copier/cruft recorded versions) fail gracefully by keeping the customer's version and leaving a visible marker (inferred). The platforms that show ownership inline on the thing (Dataverse's layer list, ServiceNow's record status) are also the ones with a per-record upgrade decision; file-based systems show it only at upgrade time via side files. No platform surveyed lets a *vendor* delete a customer-touched thing on upgrade; the most any of them does is deprecate, mark, or side-file it and leave deletion to the customer.

### Connector directories ship hash-pinned, unsigned, and auto-update is off by default in every agent-skill ecosystem

The Agent Skills shape Alpha's connectors would use has no version field. A skill is a directory with a required `SKILL.md` (YAML frontmatter plus Markdown) and optional `scripts/`, `references/`, `assets/`; the frontmatter `name` (1–64 chars, lowercase, hyphens) "must match the parent directory name", `description` is required, and the only place for provenance is the free-form `metadata` map — the spec's own example is `metadata: {author: example-org, version: "1.0"}` ([Agent Skills specification](https://agentskills.io/specification)). Loading is three-level — about 100 tokens of metadata per skill at startup, the body (under 5,000 tokens recommended) on activation, and resources only when accessed, with scripts "run through bash, and only their output enters context" ([Agent Skills overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/overview)). The standard was released as open on 18 December 2025 after the 16 October 2025 launch and lists some forty adopters (Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini CLI, GitHub Copilot, VS Code, OpenClaw, JetBrains Junie…) ([agentskills.io](https://agentskills.io/); [Skills announcement](https://claude.com/blog/skills); [Anthropic engineering post](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills)); only Anthropic's hosted `/v1/skills` endpoint versions skills server-side, with `GET /v1/skills/{skill_id}/versions/{version}/content`, and "Custom Skills do not sync across surfaces". Anthropic's security guidance is "Treat like installing software": "a malicious Skill can direct Claude to invoke tools or execute code in ways that don't match the Skill's stated purpose", audit "SKILL.md, scripts, images, and other resources", and "Skills that fetch data from external URLs pose particular risk… Even trustworthy Skills can be compromised if their external dependencies change over time" ([Agent Skills overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/overview)); the anthropics/skills repository documents only `name` and `description` as required and carries a demonstration disclaimer, with the document skills "source-available, not open source" ([github.com/anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills)). Because `name` must equal the directory name and there is no version field, a vendor must carry version and provenance in `metadata` and in a separate catalog, and two installs can only be compared by that metadata or a content hash (inferred).

Claude Code's plugin system is the closest working analogue of a vendor shipping skill directories to many installs, and its mechanics are the defaults Alpha would inherit or have to beat. Plugins launched in public beta on 9 October 2025; a marketplace is "a git repository, a GitHub repository, or a URL containing a `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` file" ([Claude Code plugins announcement](https://claude.com/blog/claude-code-plugins)); a plugin is "a directory of skills, agents, hooks, MCP servers, or other components" with a `.claude-plugin/plugin.json` manifest, and "what the plugin runs, it runs as you" ([Plugins overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins)). The marketplace file needs `name`, `owner` and a `plugins` array whose entries carry a `source` of type path, `github` (`repo`, `ref`, `sha`), `url`, `git-subdir`, `npm` ("unpacked without running install scripts"), `archive` (https-only, `sha256`, v2.1.224+) or `command` (v2.1.229+, "Claude Code shows users the whole string for review before it runs"); when a 40-character `sha` is set "Claude Code checks out `sha`" even if the ref was deleted ([Create a marketplace](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-marketplaces); [Marketplace reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/marketplace-reference)). **The version string is the sole update trigger**: "a user gets a new copy of a plugin only when its computed version changes", resolved from `plugin.json` `version`, then the marketplace entry, then a 12-character commit SHA or archive SHA-256 — so "a manifest that pins `"version": "1.0.0"` keeps every user on the cached copy until its author changes the string, however many commits they push" ([Plugin loading reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/loading)). "Background auto-update is off for your marketplace by default, and `marketplace.json` has no field to turn it on"; a user toggles it per marketplace or an admin sets `"autoUpdate": true` in managed settings; when on, "after you send your first message, Claude Code waits a random delay of up to ten minutes", refreshes, and the running session keeps its loaded versions until `/reload-plugins`; `DISABLE_UPDATES=1`, `DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER=1` or `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_NONESSENTIAL_TRAFFIC=1` switch the pass off ([Host and maintain a marketplace](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/host-marketplace); [Plugin loading reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/loading)). On disk, versioned code lives at `~/.claude/plugins/cache/<marketplace>/<plugin>/<version>/` (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}`), durable per-user data at `data/<plugin-id>/` (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}`, "keeps it across updates", deleted on uninstall), and superseded versions get an `.orphaned_at` marker and are removed after 14 days; Node dependencies install with `--ignore-scripts` from registries only, https only, in 60 seconds ([Plugin loading reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/loading)). "Claude Code has no release-channel concept, and one marketplace serves one version of each plugin at a time", so stable/early tracks are two marketplaces; renames and removals use a top-level `renames` map (to a new name or `null`) and `"forceRemoveDeletedPlugins": true` uninstalls delisted plugins at session start under **Flagged** — "there is no deprecation state"; hosted limits are 5 MiB/10 s for the marketplace file, 256 MiB/120 s/five redirects for archives, and 100,000 entries, 512 MiB per file, 1 GiB total, 50× ratio on extraction; private marketplaces use the user's own git credentials ("Claude Code has no git token of its own") or an archive `headersHelper` (v2.1.238+) minting short-lived tokens ([Host and maintain a marketplace](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/host-marketplace)). Organisations can sync a private marketplace through claude.ai so "your users' git credentials aren't involved", and managed settings offer `extraKnownMarketplaces`, `enabledPlugins`, `strictKnownMarketplaces` and `blockedMarketplaces` ([Plugin security and trust](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/security)). The trust model is explicit: "A Claude Code plugin you install can execute arbitrary code on your machine with your user privileges"; hooks, MCP servers and `bin/` run outside sandbox and permission rules; the install warning says "Anthropic does not control what MCP servers, files, or other software are included in plugins and cannot verify that they will work as intended or that they won't change"; the integrity checks that exist are reserved official/community names refused from non-`github.com/anthropics/` sources, `archive` digests ("Plugin archive integrity check failed"), and commit pins — "Where the `claude-community` catalog pins a plugin to a commit SHA, which it does for nearly every entry, Claude Code refuses to install a different commit" ([Plugin security and trust](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/security); [Marketplace reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/marketplace-reference)). Distribution routes are a bare folder (`--plugin-dir`, `--plugin-url`, `~/.claude/skills/`), your own marketplace ("Off" by default for updates), or Anthropic's directory (paid plan, auto-updates "after the version you push is published"); the pre-release checklist says "Either increment `version` on every release, or omit it in a git-hosted marketplace so Claude Code uses the commit SHA", run `claude plugin validate --strict` and `claude plugin eval`, and tag with `claude plugin tag` ([Publish and distribute a plugin](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/publish)). Recent changelog entries add `--marketplace <source>` on install (v2.1.292), claude.ai skill syncing roughly every 40 minutes when idle (v2.1.293), and refusal of skill names with hidden or direction-changing characters (v2.1.299) ([CHANGELOG.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/claude-code/main/CHANGELOG.md); release dating from [classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/20260529-cc-updates-v2-1-158/), [classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/20260813-cc-updates-v2-1-228/), [classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/20260909-cc-updates-v2-1-266/)). Two weak spots for a vendor: auto-update off by default, and no deprecation state — only rename-to-null plus forced removal (inferred).

ClawHub is the cautionary registry. It tracks "semver versions, tags such as `latest`, changelogs, files, downloads, stars, and security scan summaries", installs pin versions in `.clawhub/lock.json`, and "Each publish creates a new version record that cannot be changed afterward" with artifact digests ([ClawHub](https://docs.openclaw.ai/clawhub); [How ClawHub works](https://docs.openclaw.ai/clawhub/how-it-works)). That did not stop ClawHavoc: Koi Security's audit of all ClawHub skills (the post gives both 2,857 and 2,632 — inconsistent) found **341 malicious, 335 from one campaign** whose lure was a "Prerequisites" step pointing to a password-protected archive that defeats automated scanning and drops the Atomic Stealer, with later counts of 824 of 10,700+ ([Koi, 1 Feb 2026](https://www.koi.ai/blog/clawhavoc-341-malicious-clawedbot-skills-found-by-the-bot-they-were-targeting); timeline at [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/researchers-find-341-malicious-clawhub.html); 1,184 historically per [Antiy](https://www.antiy.net/p/clawhavoc-analysis-of-large-scale-poisoning-campaign-targeting-the-openclaw-skill-market-for-ai-agents/); near 900 per [eSecurity Planet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/hundreds-of-malicious-skills-found-in-openclaws-clawhub/)). Snyk's ToxicSkills audit (5 February 2026) of 3,984 skills found 36.82% with a security issue, 13.4% with a critical one and 76 confirmed malicious payloads, noting "The only requirement to publish a skill on ClawHub was a GitHub account at least one week old… no code signing or security review" and that "Skills inherit the agent's full permissions" ([Snyk ToxicSkills](https://snyk.io/blog/toxicskills-malicious-ai-agent-skills-clawhub/)). ClawHub then added SkillSpector, Tencent's A.I.G and its own ClawScan (statuses Pass/Review/Warn/Malicious; `.pyc` files rejected because A.I.G 0.2.1 cannot inspect bytecode, CVE-2026-84809), daily abuse checks and an appeals site ([ClawHub security audits](https://docs.openclaw.ai/clawhub/security-audits); [Moderation](https://docs.openclaw.ai/clawhub/moderation); [Security](https://docs.openclaw.ai/clawhub/security)), but Unit 42 (23 June 2026) still found five malicious skills that passed: a 22 MB-padded dropper that exceeded scanner size limits and "passed VirusTotal" while its audit was "still in review", affiliate-link redirection the publisher could change remotely, and paste-site redirects so payloads changed without republishing; ClawHub announced an NVIDIA screening partnership on 1 June 2026 ([Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/openclaw-ai-supply-chain-risk/); secondary thresholds at [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/clawhub-cli-guide/) and [Signals Corps](https://www.signalscorps.com/blog/clawhub-agent-skills-security-scan/)). The lesson is that social-engineered prerequisites, password-protected archives, oversized files and remote-fetched payloads all defeat post-hoc scanning; the defences that held were immutable versions plus digests, publisher provenance and a lockfile (inferred).

The older registries show what the agent ecosystems still lack. The MCP Registry is metadata only (preview 8 September 2025, API frozen at v0.1 on 24 October 2025), publishing proves namespace ownership via GitHub OIDC or DNS, packages must come from trusted public registries, MCPB bundles carry `fileSha256`, and `status`/`isLatest` are registry-managed fields "Publishers cannot set or override" ([modelcontextprotocol/registry](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/registry); [server.json reference](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/registry/blob/main/docs/reference/server-json/generic-server-json.md); [official registry requirements](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/registry/blob/main/docs/reference/server-json/official-registry-requirements.md)); analysts note a verified namespace "blocks impersonation, not bad data" ([Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/what-is-an-mcp-registry/); [Glama, 26 Oct 2025](https://glama.ai/blog/2025-10-26-the-model-context-protocol-registry-standardizing-server-discovery-in-a-decentralized-ecosystem)). VS Code has signed every Marketplace extension since **November 2022** and "verifies this signature when you install", refusing on failure ([VS Code v1.75 notes](https://code.visualstudio.com/updates/v1_75); [Extension runtime security](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/configure/extensions/extension-runtime-security)); Microsoft's June 2025 post describes static scans on every package, periodic rescans, sandboxed dynamic detection, a Verified Publisher badge needing domain verification plus six months' standing, and 110 of 136 reviewed extensions removed in 2025 — while publisher signing stays a roadmap item and OX Security showed a publisher can add functionality while keeping the badge, which Microsoft called "by design" ([Security and Trust in Visual Studio Marketplace](https://developer.microsoft.com/blog/security-and-trust-in-visual-studio-marketplace/); [OX Security](https://www.ox.security/blog/can-you-trust-that-verified-symbol-exploiting-ide-extensions-is-easier-than-it-should-be/); [The Hacker News, July 2025](https://thehackernews.com/2025/07/new-flaw-in-ides-like-visual-studio.html); [Sign VSIX with Sign CLI](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/visualstudio/sign-vsix-packages-with-sign-cli/)). Obsidian's registry is a git repository of JSON lists from which the app pulls `manifest.json`, `main.js` and `styles.css` "from GitHub releases tagged to match the manifest version", so "you only submit the initial version"; on 12 May 2026 it moved to automated review that "scans every version… not just the initial submission", cleared "over 2,300 queued submissions", keeps manual review for popular plugins, refuses closed source, and plans artifact attestation and access disclosures — replacing a best-effort human queue with roughly three-month waits ([obsidianmd/obsidian-releases](https://github.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-releases); [Submit your plugin](https://docs.obsidian.md/Plugins/Releasing/Submit+your+plugin); [The future of plugins](https://obsidian.md/blog/future-of-plugins/); [Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/recurrent-why-does-it-take-so-long-to-review-plugins-whats-the-usual-time-it-takes-to-review-a-new-plugin-how-long-does-it-take-to-review-a-plugin/107899)). Mature registries converge on repository-level signing of what the store serves, every-version automated scanning, and a separation between "integrity of the bytes" (signatures, pins) and "safety of the behaviour" (review, scanning); none of the agent-skill registries yet does the first (inferred).

What counts as adequate signing for scripts that run on users' machines is visible in Chrome, Homebrew, minisign, Sigstore and TUF. Chrome's CRX is signed with the developer's key whose public-key hash is the extension ID, "Updates must be signed with the same private key as the installed version", and the browser checks the update XML "Every few hours" ([Self-host for Linux](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/mv3/linux_hosting), 2017); Web Store review covers every update, usually "within a few days" but "up to a few weeks" ([Review process](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/review-process), 2021); and Manifest V3 requires that "extensions now need to bundle *all* code they are using inside the extension itself" — remotely hosted JavaScript or WebAssembly is banned, data such as JSON is not ([Remotely hosted code restrictions](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/migrate/remote-hosted-code)). Homebrew shipped build provenance for core bottles in 4.3.0 (14 May 2024) ([Trail of Bits, 2024](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2024/05/14/a-peek-into-build-provenance-for-homebrew/); [Trail of Bits, 2023](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2023/11/06/adding-build-provenance-to-homebrew/); [Homebrew 4.3.0](https://brew.sh/2024/05/14/homebrew-4.3.0/); [OpenSSF proposal](https://repos.openssf.org/proposals/build-provenance-and-code-signing-for-homebrew)); 6.0.0 (11 June 2026) made third-party taps require explicit trust because "A third-party tap can contain arbitrary, unsandboxed Ruby that runs on your machine" and fixed an HTTPS→HTTP redirect bypass and a root code execution via git hooks ([Homebrew 6.0.0](https://brew.sh/2026/06/11/homebrew-6.0.0/); [Tap Trust](https://docs.brew.sh/Tap-Trust); [Taps](https://docs.brew.sh/Taps)); 7.0.0 (13 September 2026) "verifies attestations for supported third-party tap bottles", generates attesting workflows from `brew tap-new`, delivers setup instructions "as signed data", deprecates `post_install` blocks "until 11 December 2027", adds `brew vulns`, and states that sandboxing "cannot make untrusted software safe to run" ([Homebrew 7.0.0](https://brew.sh/2026/09/13/homebrew-7.0.0/); [release](https://github.com/Homebrew/brew/releases/tag/7.0.0); [migration guide](https://brew.sh/7.0.0-migration-guide/); breakage at [LizardByte](https://github.com/LizardByte/actions/pull/302) and [ralph #216](https://github.com/lucasfe/ralph/issues/216)). Minisign (0.12) gives Ed25519 detached signatures whose trusted comment "can only be changed with the secret key" and is meant for "filenames, timestamps, resource identifiers, or version numbers to help prevent downgrade attacks" ([minisign](https://jedisct1.github.io/minisign/)); cosign signs blobs keyless against an OIDC identity with a bundle holding "the artifact's signature, its certificate, and proof of transparency log inclusion", verified with `--certificate-identity` and `--certificate-oidc-issuer` ([Signing blobs](https://docs.sigstore.dev/cosign/signing/signing_with_blobs/); [Verifying](https://docs.sigstore.dev/cosign/verifying/verify/); [cosign README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/sigstore/cosign@main/README.md); a renamed workflow breaks identity regexes per [Chainguard](https://www.chainguard.dev/fr-FR/unchained/not-all-thats-signed-is-secure-verify-the-right-way-with-tuf-and-sigstore)); and Sigstore distributes its own root of trust with TUF, whose roles — root, targets, snapshot, timestamp "re-signed often with a short expiry so clients detect being held back" — exist to "minimize the impact of key compromises" and defeat frozen, rolled-back and mix-and-match updates ([Sigstore FAQ](https://docs.sigstore.dev/about/faq/); [TUF overview](https://theupdateframework.io/overview/); [TUF roles](https://theupdateframework.io/docs/metadata/)). Judged by what these systems actually enforce, the adequate minimum for a small vendor is (inferred, no primary source states it): a per-release manifest listing every file's SHA-256 and the version/date; a detached Ed25519 signature over it (minisign with the version in the trusted comment, or a cosign bundle bound to the vendor's CI identity); the public key or expected identity pinned in the client and never fetched from the same channel; monotonic version and expiry checks so a stale manifest is refused; and https-only download with the hash checked before anything is unpacked, as Claude Code does for `archive`+`sha256`. Signing proves origin and integrity, not safety — every ecosystem pairs it with review, scanning or an explicit trust decision — and MV3's ban on remotely hosted code is the vetting precondition: a connector that fetches instructions at run time (ClawHub's `curl | source` pattern) defeats any review, so shipping complete directories and refusing runtime fetches is the vetting-compatible design.

### Site know-how fixes ship fastest from the vendor's side, and self-healing repairs stay per-customer or in drafts

How a site-know-how fix reaches users splits cleanly by where the know-how runs, and in 2025–2026 the market moved it server-side to escape client-update gates. Waalaxy declared itself "fully cloud based" on 1 July 2026, removed its Chrome extension from the Web Store, and sold the change as "Chrome updates won't break it" and the cloud version being "maintained on our end, not yours" ([Waalaxy help](https://intercom.help/waalaxy/en/articles/15587366-waalaxy-is-now-fully-cloud-based)). Apify's scheme is the most explicit "one shared version, auto-validated" model found. Builds are numbered `MAJOR.MINOR.BUILD` with tags such as `latest` or `beta` that "can be linked to only one build at a time" and are moved via the Actor API's `taggedBuilds` map ([Apify builds](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/development/builds-and-runs/builds); [Actor PUT](https://docs.apify.com/api/v2/actor-put.md)). Every self-healing framework uses the same two-tier shape — replay a recorded step with no model call, hand the failure to the model — and **none shares repairs across customers**. browser-use's Workflow Use records interactions as `.workflow.json` files, "fall[s] back to Browser Use if a step fails", and lists "Self healing, if it fails automatically agent kicks in and updates the workflow file" as roadmap, with the README calling the LLM fallback "currently really bad" and the project not for production (last push 9 October 2026, 4,225 stars) ([workflow-use](https://github.com/browser-use/workflow-use); [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/browser-use/workflow-use)). Stagehand v2 cached `observe()` results locally keyed by prompt and replayed them with "NO LLM INFERENCE", retrying with the natural-language prompt when self-heal was on ([Stagehand v2 caching](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v2/best-practices/caching)); v3 added `selfHeal` default true and `serverCache` only on Browserbase ([v3 reference](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v3/references/stagehand.md)); v4 moved the cache server-side, keyed on "the instruction, page content, and the options you pass", with a `threshold` of identical results before serving hits, `metadata.cache.status` HIT/MISS/DISABLED, and the rules "Changes to page content or structure cause a miss", "If the recorded selector no longer resolves and `selfHeal` is on, Stagehand re-infers the action and retries once", and — the one place vendor-side caching can leak customer data — "Variable values are sent to the cache service, so calls that carry credentials should set `cache: false`" ([v4 caching](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v4/best-practices/caching); [v4 act()](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v4/basics/act)). Skyvern generates code from a successful agent run; "If the cached code fails, for example because the page changed, Skyvern falls back to the agent and regenerates the code", caching per workflow block, and its v1.0.47 release (16 July, year inferred 2026) added "schema-first storage for heal episodes and heal proposals", a reliability aggregation service and a reliability badge, with recovery "behind `enable_self_healing`" ([Skyvern code caching](https://skyvern.com/docs/developers/features/code-caching); [docs-new](https://docs-new.skyvern.com/docs/features/code-caching); [v1.0.47](https://github.com/Skyvern-AI/skyvern/releases/tag/v1.0.47); [mirror](https://newreleases.io/project/github/Skyvern-AI/skyvern/release/v1.0.47)). Anchor Browser separates an in-run "AI fallback" from post-run "Workflow self-healing" that "proposes an updated workflow" saved as a draft — "Nothing goes live automatically. You review the draft, test it, and deploy it" — signalled by a `task.healed` webhook with a `confidence_score` and approved via `POST /v2/tasks/{taskId}/publish-draft` ([Anchor self-healing](https://docs.anchorbrowser.io/advanced/self-healing)). Magnitude's vision-first agent avoids the DOM entirely and lists deterministic caching as "in progress" ([magnitudedev/browser-agent](https://github.com/magnitudedev/browser-agent); [magnitudedev/magnitude](https://github.com/magnitudedev/magnitude)). UiPath's Healing Agent (22 July 2025) offers a "self-healing mode" that applies fixes and a "recommendation mode" that records failures and suggests fixes "developers can review and apply", chosen by administrators, with no statement on sharing fixes across tenants ([UiPath Technical Tuesday](https://www.uipath.com/blog/product-and-updates/technical-tuesday-how-healing-agent-solves-ui-automation-challenges)); generic auto-healing guides warn that "a subtle UI change several steps earlier may cause the wrong element to be clicked" ([BrowserStack](https://www.browserstack.com/guide/auto-healing-automation)). The norm that emerges is "repair goes local first, a human approves promotion" for anything that writes back to a shared definition (Anchor drafts, UiPath recommendation mode, Skyvern's proposals table), while automatic in-place regeneration is confined to per-customer caches where a bad repair hurts only that customer; Anchor's webhook-plus-publish API is the closest primitive on which a vendor could build a repair-sharing loop, but nobody has (inferred). Whether Stagehand's server-side cache is scoped per API key or shared across customers is not stated in its docs.

Testing a connector for a sign-in-bound site without a sandbox is an acknowledged blind spot. The generic pattern is to keep parameters in the input, credentials in the user's store, and only selectors and procedures in the connector — Apify's `prefill` defaults, Claude Code's versioned `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}` versus durable `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}`, Stagehand's `cache: false` for credentialed calls ([Actor testing](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/publishing/test); [Plugin loading reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/loading); [v4 act()](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v4/basics/act)) — and to detect breakage by "did the step run and did it return rows" rather than by selector inspection or screenshot comparison, which no vendor documents (inferred). Playwright can record real traffic to a HAR with `page.routeFromHAR(path, { update: true })`, commit the scrubbed file and replay it strictly by URL and method ([Playwright: Mock APIs](https://playwright.dev/docs/mock)); Octoparse's check is "re-run the template with one or two known inputs and compare the output" ([Octoparse troubleshooting](https://www.octoparse.com/docs/en/platform/templates/troubleshooting.md)). No vendor publicly documents how it tests LinkedIn, Gmail or Outlook-web connectors, and Apify's thresholds (non-empty dataset, five minutes, 3/14/14 days) are the only published ones; a realistic plan is scrubbed HAR/DOM fixtures from a real session for offline tests, a live canary on the vendor's own account at human pace, and per-install health from each person's own runs reported back as counts rather than content (inferred).

### Integration platforms version the definition, bind credentials per customer, and let the customer's required action decide whether a release is silent

Every embedded iPaaS examined separates a vendor-owned, versioned definition from a per-customer deployed copy that carries only that customer's configuration values and credentials, entered through a wizard and never part of the published artifact. Prismatic: "An instance of an integration is a copy of an integration that has been configured for a specific customer"; publishing creates a new integration version, an updatable instance shows "New Version Available", updating means **Reconfigure** and choosing the version, instances can run different versions at once, and rollback is "always reconfigure the instance to run an older version" ([Prismatic: Instances](https://www.prismatic.io/docs/instances); [Deploying instances](https://prismatic.io/docs/instances/deploying/)). Its connection scopes — Customer ("Your customers will enter their own credentials"), Organization (Customer), Organization (Global) and User ("each user's connection is visible only to them") — and its changelog from "Enhanced Versioning" (16 June 2021) through "Headless configuration" (14 October 2026) show the model maturing in exactly this direction ([Connections](https://prismatic.io/docs/connections/); [changelog, deployment tag](https://prismatic.io/docs/changelog/tags/deployment/)). Tray quantifies the silent case: with no breaking changes "the instance updates automatically, with no user action" in "approx 2 minutes from release" (a "lazy update"), while an auth that gains scopes, moves to a new service version or changes environment, or a config slot whose type or linked auth changes, is breaking; flags `hasNewerVersion`, `requiresUserInputToUpdateVersion` and `requiresSystemInputToUpdateVersion` tell the vendor whether nobody, the end user, or the vendor's own `upgradeSolutionInstance` call must act, and a hotfix that recreates an auth slot "can falsely flag a breaking change even when the scopes match" unless the same `authId` is passed ([Tray: Solution Instances](https://tray.ai/documentation/platform/embedded/key-concepts/solution-instances); [Tray: change management](https://tray.ai/documentation/platform/enterprise-core/lifecycle-management/embedded-release-management/change-management); [legacy tray.io](https://tray.io/documentation/embedded/building-integrations/solution-instances/); [Project versioning](https://tray.ai/documentation/platform/enterprise-core/lifecycle-management/project-versioning)). Paragon's Releases make Staging and Production read-only, require a semantic version, reject stale previews, and classify Release Messages into Warnings that block ("New Integration Requires Additional App Configuration", "Environment Secret Missing") and Informational effects on existing users — "OAuth App Scopes Changed" (users must disconnect and reconnect; affected users exportable), "Required User Setting Added", "Integration Removed" ("all connected accounts and workflows associated with this integration will be deleted"); rollback is not documented (the launch post, bylined 11 October 2023 but carrying October 2026 page metadata, listed it as planned) ([Paragon: Release Environments](https://docs.useparagon.com/deploying-integrations/release-environments); [Paragon blog](https://www.useparagon.com/blog/launching-releases)). Workato's embedded RLCM exports a manifest/package in which "Only the connection name, application type and other non-sensitive information will be exported", creates placeholder connections on import that must be re-authenticated, overwrites recipes by name, and reports per recipe `no_update_or_update_without_restart` versus `restarted` ([Workato Embedded RLCM](https://docs.workato.com/oem/oem-api/recipe-lifecycle-management); [Workato Importing](https://docs.workato.com/recipe-development-lifecycle/import.html); [Embedded implementation guide](https://docs.workato.com/en/oem/workato-embedded-implementation.html); a user who lost environment-variable credentials on import at [Workato community](https://systematic.workato.com/t5/workato-pros-discussion-board/workato-manifest-deployment-and-http-connection-context/m-p/5865)). Cyclr never updates automatically: each connector update is a versioned Connector Release, installed connectors and template steps "are not automatically updated", upgrading a connector instance clears authentication, template releases go Draft → Live → Deprecated, "Deleting a Template will NOT affect Cycles… installed from the Template", and "Any settings or credentials stored on a Template Connector will NOT be copied over" ([Cyclr: connector releases](https://community.cyclr.com/user-documentation/connectors/handling-connector-releases); [Templates](https://community.cyclr.com/user-documentation/templates/templates); [Template versioning](https://community.cyclr.com/user-documentation/templates/template-versioning); [Connector installation](https://community.cyclr.com/user-documentation/connectors/connector-installation-overview)). Pandium makes the choice per integration — Static Default (tenants never move), Dynamic Default (move on next sync when the default changes), Latest (always newest) — with the caveat that new config defaults "will not reflect" in existing tenants ([Pandium: releases](https://docs.pandium.com/integration-hub/updating-an-integrations-release); [bulk actions](https://docs.pandium.com/admin-101/bulk-actions); [tenants](https://docs.pandium.com/integration-hub/managing-and-updating-tenants-in-the-admin-portal); [July 2023 update](https://www.pandium.com/blogs/pandium-product-updates-july-2023)). Nango and Merge use the same split in unified-API form: "Connections are instances of integrations for individual users or entities. They store the authentication credentials" and a Linked Account ID "stays the same for the life of the account" ([Nango core concepts](https://docs.nango.dev/understand/core-concepts); [Merge Linked Account](https://help.merge.dev/articles/5518566-what-is-a-merge-linked-account)). No platform merges customer customisations into a new vendor version automatically; customer-side customisation survives only when it lives in the config layer, and "re-run the wizard" is the universal UX for the change case (inferred).

The data-connector and API vendors turn the same idea into dated policy. Airbyte Cloud: "Minor and Patch Versions: These are applied automatically and immediately… You don't need to take any action"; a major "will include notable changes that affect your schema or sync success", comes with a notice window, and "At the end of the window, we will automatically upgrade your connector" — though an alternate documentation path says syncs are instead *paused* at the deadline, a disagreement the notes could not resolve ([Airbyte: Managing connector updates](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/managing-airbyte/connector-updates); [alternate path](https://docs.airbyte.com/managing-airbyte/connector-updates); [1.6 docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/1.6/managing-airbyte/connector-updates)). Self-managed users opt in, can pin a version, and are told to review majors per connection. Fivetran classifies its own schema migrations Red (renames, key and type changes), Yellow (discontinuations) and Green (additions), applies additions "automatically… no schema update windows", publishes release notes with a change table and ERD, emails the application date, and ends one-month windows by applying the rest automatically (Rootly: window 21 January–21 February 2025; Attentive: nine possible breaking changes effective 18 September 2024) ([Fivetran: Schema migration](https://fivetran.com/docs/using-fivetran/features/schema-migration), search snippet — the page 404'd; [Rootly notes](https://fivetran.com/docs/connectors/applications/rootly/changelog); [Attentive notes](https://fivetran.com/docs/connectors/applications/attentive/changelog); settings at [Connection schema](https://fivetran.com/docs/using-fivetran/fivetran-dashboard/connectors/schema); [Schema migrations concepts](https://fivetran.com/docs/core-concepts/schema-migrations)); deleted source columns are never dropped, only nulled forward ([Why deleted columns still appear](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/troubleshooting/column-deleted-from-source-is-in-destination); [Fivetran blog, 2019](https://www.fivetran.com/blog/schema-changes)). Stripe is the strictest never-silently-change model: since 2024-09-30.acacia "we release new API versions monthly with no breaking changes. Twice a year, we issue a new major release… You can safely upgrade to any monthly release without updating your code"; the current version is 2026-09-30.endive, each account has a default version it upgrades in Workbench, each request can pin `Stripe-Version`, SDK majors are bound to the API version current at release, and event destinations carry their own `snapshot_api_version` with a documented blue/green webhook migration ([Stripe: API versioning](https://docs.stripe.com/api/versioning); [Stripe: Upgrade your integration](https://docs.stripe.com/upgrades); [Stripe blog, 1 Oct 2024](https://stripe.com/blog/introducing-stripes-new-api-release-process)). n8n keeps old definitions alive inside existing artefacts: "If a user builds and saves a workflow using version 1, n8n continues to use version 1 in that workflow, even if you create and publish a version 2 of the node", new workflows get the latest, deprecated nodes "continue to run" but get no fixes until removal, and n8n 1.0 (24 July 2023) kept the legacy execution order for existing workflows while other changes did alter them ([Node versioning](https://docs.n8n.io/integrations/creating-nodes/build/reference/node-versioning/); [Deprecated and versioned nodes](https://docs.n8n.io/integrations/builtin/deprecated-and-versioned-nodes/); [1.0 migration checklist](https://docs.n8n.io/1-0-migration-checklist/); the undocumented "add a fresh node" upgrade path per [Lime CRM](https://platform.docs.lime-crm.com/en/latest/workflows-and-integrations/node-versioning/), unverified). Zapier enforces semver by detection — removed keys, auth-type and input-type changes "block promotion" without a major bump — allows automatic user migration only within a major, in staged percentages from 5% to 100% with monitoring, and requires a deprecation date "between 3 weeks and 1 year in the future" with 14-day and day-of emails, after which "Zap workflows that haven't been updated will be automatically paused"; "Hiding is not a breaking change", end users never see a version number, and templates carry steps but "do not include any values you've entered into fields" or connected accounts (shared templates themselves retire on 15 December 2026) ([Zapier: Versions](https://docs.zapier.com/platform/manage/versions); [Planning changes](https://docs.zapier.com/platform/manage/planning-changes); [Migrate users](https://docs.zapier.com/platform/manage/migrate); [Deprecate](https://docs.zapier.com/platform/manage/deprecate); [Guided template](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/44821650010637); [Share a template](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496292155405); Make's equivalents at [Scenario templates](https://help.make.com/scenario-templates) and [Connecting to services](https://www.make.com/en/help/connections/connecting-to-services)).

### On macOS in 2026, Developer ID plus notarization is the floor, TCC follows the signing identity, and MDM can pre-grant what users would otherwise be asked

Direct distribution now means an Apple Developer Program membership, a Developer ID Application certificate, hardened runtime, a secure timestamp and notarization — and the Control-click escape hatch is gone. "Beginning in macOS 10.15, all software built after June 1, 2019, and distributed with Developer ID must be notarized"; notarization "is not App Review", "usually takes less than an hour", requires `notarytool` or Xcode 14+ since 1 November 2023, forbids ad hoc, development or Mac Distribution certificates, and gives the vendor a recovery path — "if your Developer ID signing key is exposed… you can work with Apple to revoke the tickets associated with those versions" ([Apple: Notarizing macOS software](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/notarizing-macos-software-before-distribution); [Hardened Runtime](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/hardened-runtime); [Developer ID](https://developer.apple.com/developer-id/)). Membership is "99 USD per membership year"; organisations "must be a legal entity that can enter into contracts with Apple. We do not accept DBAs, fictitious business names, trade names, or branches", need a D-U-N-S Number, a domain email and a working website ([Apple: Enrollment](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)); only the Account Holder creates Developer ID certificates, up to five of each kind, and "If your membership expires, users can still download, install, and run your applications" but new certificates need active membership; a revoked certificate stops installs of everything signed with it and "Apple can revoke digital certificates at any time at its sole discretion" ([Create Developer ID certificates](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/certificates/create-developer-id-certificates/); [Certificates support](https://developer.apple.com/support/certificates/)). The policy change that binds: Apple announced on 6 August 2024 that "In macOS Sequoia, users will no longer be able to Control-click to override Gatekeeper when opening software that isn't signed correctly or notarized. They'll need to visit System Settings > Privacy & Security to review security information for software before allowing it to run" ([Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=saqachfa)); Apple's support page (27 May 2026) shows a non-notarized app met with "Apple cannot check 'Example App' for malicious software" and Move to Trash/Done, with the only route being try once, then Privacy & Security › "Open Anyway", then Open again, after which the app "is then saved as an exception" — and no "Anywhere" option in the Gatekeeper popup ([Apple: Safely open apps on your Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102445)). On Tahoe, Gatekeeper runs signature, notarization-ticket and CDHash checks plus a first-run XProtect scan that "may be skipped if the first three checks pass", `spctl --master-disable` or an `EnableAssessment=false` profile "only lets you run signed apps that aren't notarised", and there is "no direct way to disable Gatekeeper, on-demand XProtect, or XProtect Remediator scans" ([Eclectic Light, 6 Aug 2026](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/08/06/can-you-disable-gatekeeper-and-xprotect/); launch-time measurements at [30 Sep 2025](https://eclecticlight.co/2025/09/30/do-apps-launch-faster-in-macos-tahoe/) and [13 Oct 2025](https://eclecticlight.co/2025/10/13/is-tahoe-quicker-to-launch-apps-first-time/)). No official notarization or Gatekeeper *policy* change specific to macOS 26 was found; the Tahoe items are third-party measurements and bypass fixes (CVE-2025-46291 fixed in 26.2; CVE-2026-20684, an AppleScript permissions bypass, fixed in 26.4 and disclosed 24 March 2026) ([SentinelOne](https://www.sentinelone.com/vulnerability-database/cve-2025-46291/); [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/vulnerability-database/cve/cve-2026-20684)), plus an unanswered forum report of a notarized, stapled `.pkg` rejected on 26.3 while `.app` bundles passed ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/tags/gatekeeper)). The consequence for a fleet of client Macs: a self-signed certificate cannot be notarized, so every first launch on Sequoia or Tahoe needs the Open Anyway dance per Mac, and the "identified developers" setting does not help (inferred).

TCC grants are keyed to the code-signing designated requirement, which is why the signing identity must be stable before any client grants anything. Apple's TN3127 (May 2022, revised 2 April 2024): "Most code has a designated requirement (DR) which is how the code identifies itself"; after a user authorises microphone access to version 1.2 and the updater installs 1.3, "macOS solves this problem by recording your app's DR in its database of apps authorized to access the microphone. Each time your app tries to access the microphone, macOS checks that this version of the app satisfies the original DR"; "Unsigned code has no DR. Ad hoc signed code… has a DR but it's tied to that specific version of the code. In both cases macOS can't reliably track the identity of the code"; and an Apple Development build is not compatible with a Developer ID build — "the system will display a prompt" when switching ([Apple TN3127](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3127-inside-code-signing-requirements)). Quinn of Apple DTS (post revised 18 September 2026) says the same for file-system permissions: "If your code is unsigned, or signed ad hoc…, the system can't tell that version N+1 of your code is the same as version N, and thus you'll encounter excessive prompts"; some privileges are per user (Files and Folders) and some system-wide (Full Disk Access); "On managed systems the site admin can use the `com.apple.TCC.configuration-profile-policy` payload to assign MAC privileges"; and the list of protected locations "is not documented and… can change over time" ([On File System Permissions](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/678819)). The database stores a compiled requirement in its `csreq` column — a blob derived from `codesign -d -r-` — "used to prevent spoofing/impersonation if another program uses the same bundle identifier" ([HackTricks: macOS TCC](https://book.hacktricks.xyz/macos-hardening/macos-security-and-privilege-escalation/macos-security-protections/macos-tcc); [Jamf Nation](https://community.jamf.com/general-discussions-2/how-to-get-tcc-database-csreq-blob-programmatically-12684)). The live failure of this kind is Anthropic's own: claude-code issue #57679 (opened 9 May 2026, closed 11 June 2026 "not planned") reports that "Every time the Claude Code CLI auto-updates, macOS prompts again for Full Disk Access" because each version under `~/.local/share/claude/versions/X.Y.Z` has "a distinct code-signing identity", granting FDA to Terminal "does not help", and the proposed fix is "Sign all CLI version binaries with a stable code-signing identity" ([issue #57679 mirror](https://claudeissues.com/issue/57679-macos-fda-permission-prompt-recurs-after-every-cli-update)). Two design consequences follow (inferred): a self-signed certificate kept constant across versions does give persistent grants, because the DR is certificate-based rather than cdhash-based, but the first Developer ID build has a different DR and will re-prompt every Calendar, folder and FDA grant — so the switch should happen once, early, before the fleet grows; and Apple's revocation power is both the recovery path after key compromise and a single point where new installs can be bricked, so the Developer ID private key belongs offline or in Apple's cloud-managed signing.

Apple's trust chain has failed in documented ways that all point at the same controls. KeRanger (4–5 March 2016) shipped inside Transmission 2.90 installers on the official site, "signed with a legitimate Apple-issued Mac app development certificate" from an unrelated Turkish company (developer ID `Z7276PX673`), which Apple revoked ([Unit 42, Mar 2016](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/new-os-x-ransomware-keranger-infected-transmission-bittorrent-client-installer/)); HandBrake 1.0.7 was replaced by a malicious copy on 2 May 2017 and "HandBrake is not code signed, so there's no real way to verify" ([Malwarebytes, 8 May 2017](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2017/05/handbrake-hacked-to-drop-new-variant-of-proton-malware)); Shlayer adware "managed to get their payloads notarized" on 28 August 2020 ([Security Intelligence](https://securityintelligence.com/news/apple-malware-tricks-apple-notarization-mechanism-for-the-first-time); [Red Canary](https://redcanary.com/threat-detection-report/threats/Shlayer/)); the trojanised 3CX Desktop App (29 March 2023, Electron) was "validly signed by the 3CX developer" and notarized, with the payload in `libffmpeg.dylib` ([Objective-See](https://objective-see.org/blog/blog_0x73.html)); and Zoom's privileged macOS auto-updater could be made to install a downgraded or different package (CVE-2022-28756, CWE-347, CVSS 8.8, fixed in 5.11.5, bulletin 13 August 2022) ([Zoom ZSB-22018](https://www.zoom.com/en/trust/security-bulletin/ZSB-22018/); [MITRE](https://cveawg.mitre.org/api/cve/CVE-2022-28756)). Apple's own private channel exists but is App Review-gated: Custom Apps are visible only to organisations named by Organization ID in Apple Business Manager and distributed by their MDM or redemption codes, a choice "only available before your app has been approved" ([Custom apps](https://developer.apple.com/business/custom-apps/); [developer page](https://developer.apple.com/custom-apps/)); whether macOS is covered was not confirmed from the fetched pages.

For a client firm's fleet, MDM can pre-grant what each person would otherwise be asked, and managed preferences give the vendor a clean firm-level settings layer. The `com.apple.TCC.configuration-profile-policy` payload (macOS 10.14+, device channel, "Requires user-approved MDM") identifies an app by bundle ID or path plus a `CodeRequirement` taken from `codesign -dr -`, and Apple's deployment guide lists the services it can allow — Accessibility, AppleEvents, Calendar, Contacts, Desktop/Documents/Downloads folders, Photos, Reminders, System Policy All Files — while **Camera, Microphone and Screen Recording can only be denied**; with several payloads "the operating system uses the more restrictive settings", the device must be enrolled and supervised, and a `log stream` predicate on `com.apple.TCC` identifies the binary behind persistent prompts ([Apple: PrivacyPreferencesPolicyControl](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicemanagement/privacypreferencespolicycontrol); [Apple Platform Deployment: PPPC payload, 29 Jul 2024](https://support.apple.com/guide/deployment/privacy-preferences-policy-control-payload-dep38df53c2a/web); Jamf's open-source builder at [jamf/PPPC-Utility](https://github.com/jamf/PPPC-Utility)). Preferences resolve through a search list where "the value is taken from the domain nearest the beginning", management "may force preference values on the user" that "are also picked up by the CFPreferencesCopyAppValue API", and "For managed keys, you should disable any user interface that allows the user to modify the value for the key" via `CFPreferencesAppValueIsForced`, a concept dating to OS X 10.2 ([Preference domains, archived](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Cocoa/Conceptual/UserDefaults/AboutPreferenceDomains/AboutPreferenceDomains.html); [CFPreferences best practices, archived](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/CoreFoundation/Conceptual/CFPreferences/Concepts/BestPractices.html); [Custom settings payload](https://help.apple.com/profilemanager/mac/5.0/en.lproj/apd9ABBDBE7-E899-4EF8-A087-CC771AE6729A.html); forced versus set-once keys in [ManagedPreferences](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicemanagement/managedpreferences) and an [iMazing guide](https://imazing.com/guides/working-with-managed-client-mcx-configuration-profile-payloads), secondary). Chrome is the worked example: Keystone update policies in `com.google.Keystone.plist` under `/Library/Managed Preferences/` (`UpdateDefault` 0 = "always applied when detected", `TargetVersionPrefix` pinning that "should be temporary", `RollbackToTargetVersion` to one of the three latest majors, `TargetChannel`), and since Chrome 73 "any users that have enterprise policies set in Chrome will see an indicator in the action menu telling them that Chrome is being managed" ([Manage Chrome updates (Mac)](https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/7591084); [Chrome Enterprise release 73](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/chrome-enterprise/admin-insider-whats-new-in-chrome-enterprise-release-73/); [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/?p=151022)). The free or near-free MDM tiers small firms can use are Apple Business Manager, Mosyle Business Free ("the complete… feature set… for up to 30 devices at no charge", 5 April 2021; Premium about $1/device/month with a 30-licence minimum), Jamf Now (three devices free, then roughly $4/device/month — a 2020 listing says $2, so sources conflict), and in the US Apple Business Essentials (announced November 2021, "$2.99 to $24.99 per month per worker", for businesses "with up to 500 employees"; the Essentials URL now redirects to a general Apple Business page with no pricing) ([Mosyle](https://mosyle.com/news-room/mosyle-launches-mosyle-business-free); [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/183343/Mosyle-Business/pricing/); [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/mobility/jamf-now-profile/); [Macworld](https://shop.macworld.com/sales/manage-up-to-3-devices-for-free-with-jamf-now); [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/business-operations/business-management/361508/apple-unveils-business-essentials-for-smbs); [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/enterprise-software/news/252509743/Apple-launches-SMB-device-management?amp=1); [Coverager](https://coverager.com/apple-business-essentials-now-available-for-small-businesses/); [business.apple.com](https://business.apple.com/)). No current survey of MDM adoption among 10–20-Mac firms exists; the only numbers are 2012–2014 and not Mac-specific (17% of SMBs actively using MDM in 2013; about a quarter per IDC in 2014) ([Computerworld, 2013](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1422374/mobile-device-management-missing-in-small-businesses.html); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/fr/small-business/small-businesses-embrace-byod-lacking-on-mdm-adoption/); [Sophos, 2014](https://blogs.sophos.com/2014/01/31/idc-survey-sophos-mobile-control-most-widely-used-in-the-smb-market/)), so the design must handle both cases: a PPPC pre-grant where MDM exists, and a guided self-grant where it does not (inferred). The clean split for the vendor's own settings is the macOS one — user-level defaults in the app domain, firm-level values as forced keys from MDM, read through CFPreferences and surfaced as locked with a "managed by your organisation" indicator (inferred).

Sparkle 2 is the reference design for a direct-distribution Mac updater and shows every knob a small vendor needs. The feed is a static RSS "appcast" over HTTPS; every archive carries an EdDSA (ed25519) `sparkle:edSignature` and `length`, and since 2.9.0 (22 February 2026) `SURequireSignedFeed` can require the appcast and release notes to be signed too; items gate on `sparkle:minimumSystemVersion`, `sparkle:minimumUpdateVersion` (2.9+, so only users on 1.9+ see 2.0), `sparkle:minimumAutoupdateVersion` (lower versions always see the update dialog) and `sparkle:hardwareRequirements`; `<sparkle:criticalUpdate>` items are "shown more promptly and cannot be skipped"; informational items only link; channels (`<sparkle:channel>beta</sparkle:channel>`, "for temporary branches, not parallel products") are opted into by the app's delegate; phased rollout hard-codes **seven groups** released one `sparkle:phasedRolloutInterval` apart from `pubDate`, with each client drawing a random group stored locally and "not sent to servers", and "Phased rollouts do not apply to critical updates"; delta updates fall back to the full archive on failure ([Sparkle: Publishing](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/publishing/); [Delta updates](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/delta-updates/); [2.9.0 release](https://api.github.com/repos/sparkle-project/Sparkle/releases/tags/2.9.0); 2.10.0 of 13 September 2026 requires macOS 12 and 2026 point releases hardened symlink, delta and installer handling per [latest release](https://api.github.com/repos/sparkle-project/Sparkle/releases/latest) and [releases](https://github.com/sparkle-project/Sparkle/releases)). Client defaults matter for the silent-versus-prompted question: `SUEnableAutomaticChecks` unset asks on the second launch, `SUScheduledCheckInterval` is a day (minimum an hour), **`SUAutomaticallyUpdate` defaults to NO** (when YES, updates "download and install silently in the background", though "installation may not happen automatically if authorization is required"), the "impatient" interval after which a ready update may be surfaced is a week, `SUVerifyUpdateBeforeExtraction` and `SURequireSignedFeed` default to NO, and `SUSignedFeedFailureExpirationInterval` defaults to 20 days after which "updates are presented anyway as a failsafe" — an availability-over-security trade a vendor wanting freeze-attack protection should set to 0 ([Sparkle: Customization](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/customization/)). The delegate exposes `feedURLStringForUpdater:`, `allowedChannelsForUpdater:`, `updater:willDownloadUpdate:withRequest:` (where auth headers go), `decryptionPasswordForUpdater:` for encrypted archives, and veto/postpone hooks ([SPUUpdaterDelegate](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/api-reference/Protocols/SPUUpdaterDelegate.html)); Sparkle recommends Developer ID notarization on top of its own signature and warns it "will not notify your user if an update cannot be performed" by default ([Sparkle: Documentation](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/)). Its one famous failure is instructive: in January 2016 apps fetching the appcast over plain HTTP (Adium, Coda, iTerm, Sequel Pro, VLC, Camtasia, Sketch…) could be hijacked by a man-in-the-middle serving release notes with JavaScript, "The vulnerability is not in code signing itself", and the fix was 1.13.1 plus HTTPS feeds — while XQuartz could not upgrade because it still supported Snow Leopard ([vulnsec, Jan 2016](https://vulnsec.com/2016/osx-apps-vulnerabilities/); [mjtsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=13402); [freedesktop bug](https://bugs.freedesktop.org/show_bug.cgi?id=94039); [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2016/02/apple-security-vulnerability.html)). Electron's `autoUpdater` on macOS is Squirrel.Mac: the app "must be signed", the server returns JSON with `url` (plus optional `sha256`, `size`, `delta`) or `204 No Content`, a download failing its size/SHA-256 check "is discarded before it is opened", the update applies when the app quits, and the new bundle must satisfy the running app's designated requirement (`SQRLCodeSignature`) — the server "can drive behaviors like rolling back or phased rollouts" but there is no client rollback ([Electron: autoUpdater](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/auto-updater); [Squirrel.Mac README](https://github.com/Squirrel/Squirrel.Mac); [SQRLCodeSignature.h](https://github.com/Squirrel/Squirrel.Mac/blob/master/Squirrel/SQRLCodeSignature.h)); hosting options are update.electronjs.org (public GitHub repos only), static S3-style storage, or Hazel/Nuts/Nucleus, and `update-electron-app` checks "at app startup, then every ten minutes" ([update.electronjs.org](https://github.com/electron/update.electronjs.org); [Electron: Updating applications](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/updates)); electron-builder's updater uses `latest-mac.yml` with `sha512`, hand-edited `stagingPercentage` for staged rollout ("you **must** increment the version number higher than your broken release" to withdraw one), `requestHeaders`/`addAuthHeader` for private feeds sent "only to the feed's origin", and carries two relevant advisories — a Windows code-signing bypass (9 July 2024) and a cross-origin redirect that leaked `PRIVATE-TOKEN`/`Authorization` credentials (16 June 2026) ([electron-builder: Auto Update](https://www.electron.build/docs/features/auto-update); [advisories](https://github.com/electron-userland/electron-builder/security/advisories); [Keygen note on CVE-2024-39698](https://keygen.sh/docs/api/auto-updates/)). Tauri 2's updater makes signing mandatory ("cannot be disabled"), ships `myapp.app.tar.gz` plus `.sig`, pins `pubkey` in the app, takes an `endpoints` array with failover "only… if a non-2XX status code is returned", enforces TLS in production, supports `{{current_version}}`/`{{target}}`/`{{arch}}` placeholders, `headers` for auth, runtime endpoint changes for channels, and refuses downgrades unless `version_comparator` is overridden; losing the private key means never updating existing users again; RustSec lists no advisories against the updater, and whether its signatures are minisign-compatible was not confirmed ([Tauri 2: Updater](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/updater/); [RustSec: tauri](https://rustsec.org/packages/tauri.html)).

Hosting is plain HTTPS static storage, and "private" feeds are gated by tokens or signed URLs, never by hiding the URL. Cindori's release scripts (6 February 2025) notarise and staple a DMG, run `generate_appcast`, upload to S3 and invalidate CloudFront "so the update is available immediately", having moved because "App Center is shutting down its services, including Sparkle update hosting" ([Cindori](https://cindori.com/developer/automating-sparkle-updates); [App Center legacy docs](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-us/appcenter/distribution/sparkleupdates)); Sparkle's maintainers hold that feed and download URLs "will always be visible to anyone who inspects the app, so the security model should not depend on hiding them" ([Sparkle discussion #2301](https://github.com/sparkle-project/Sparkle/discussions/2301); [Glitter](https://github.com/ayumi/glitter)); CloudFront's answer is signed URLs or signed cookies with trusted key groups and origin access control ([AWS: Serve private content](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonCloudFront/latest/DeveloperGuide/PrivateContent.html)); Keygen gates Sparkle, Electron and Tauri update checks with `Authorization: License <key>` or `Bearer <token>` headers and warns "never embed admin, environment, or product tokens in client code" ([Keygen: Auto-updates](https://keygen.sh/docs/api/auto-updates/)); Sketch's licence is "perpetual and includes one year's worth of updates", $120 per seat per year to renew, after which "your last supported Sketch version" is what the feed serves ([Sketch: expired license](https://www.sketch.com/support/mac-only-licenses/expired-license/); [Sketch: license](https://sketch.com/license)); historic requests for HTTP Basic auth in Sparkle were local patches ([Launchpad Q78032](https://answers.launchpad.net/sparkle/+question/78032); [Q49805](https://answers.launchpad.net/sparkle/+question/49805)). No public example of a Mac vendor serving per-firm add-on feeds was found, no vendor documents encrypting artifacts to a per-install key, and the practical pattern is a public signed index for the app plus per-customer entries whose download URLs are short-lived signed URLs or paths behind a signed cookie — confidentiality from the URL, integrity never from it (inferred).

Channel security has a checklist (TUF) and two proven static implementations (apt, and by negative example Homebrew). TUF's threat list — arbitrary installation, rollback, fast-forward, indefinite freeze, endless data, mix-and-match, key compromise — is met by expiring metadata, monotonic version integers, hashes and lengths for every target, offline root and targets keys with an online timestamp key, and consistent snapshots; spec v1.0.37 (8 October 2026) says clients "MUST NOT trust an expired file" and must never accept lower metadata versions ([TUF: Security](https://theupdateframework.io/security/); [TUF specification](https://theupdateframework.github.io/specification/latest/); Sigstore's model at [Sigstore overview](https://docs.sigstore.dev/about/overview/)). apt's `InRelease` is clear-signed with `Date` and `Valid-Until`, lists SHA-256 and size for every index, serves indexes by hash, and gates automatic installation with `NotAutomatic`/`ButAutomaticUpgrades` — though "Client behaviour on expired Release files is unspecified" ([Debian Repository Format](https://wiki.debian.org/DebianRepository/Format)); the 2008 study that motivated TUF found all ten package managers exploitable by a mirror or man-in-the-middle through replay and freeze, after obtaining official mirrors "using false credentials" ([Cappos et al., CCS 2008](https://www2.cs.arizona.edu/stork/packagemanagersecurity/ccs2008.pdf); [LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/327847); [;login:](https://www.cs.arizona.edu/~jsamuel/papers/samuel-cappos-login-feb09.pdf)). Homebrew's 2021 incident — an auto-merge Action whose diff parser could be fooled so "An attacker could have injected arbitrary code into a cask and had it merged automatically" (reported 18 April, disclosed 21 April 2021) — is the argument against a git repository as an integrity boundary ([Homebrew incident disclosure](https://brew.sh/2021/04/21/security-incident-disclosure/)). The minimal static design assembled from these sources (inferred; no source audits it end to end): one signed index per channel with a strictly increasing serial and issued date, an `expires` date a few weeks out refreshed on a schedule even without releases, each artifact's path, length and SHA-256, and the minimum client version allowed to install; client rules that reject older serials, expired indexes, oversized downloads, hash mismatches and any artifact not listed; a minisign/ed25519 signature per artifact whose trusted comment carries file name, version and build date; Apple code-signing and notarization of every bundle with a Squirrel-style designated-requirement check of the new bundle; offline index and artifact keys with a documented rotation path; hash- or version-prefixed file names so CDN caches never mix an old index with new artifacts; and HTTPS-only URLs.

The evidence on silent versus prompted updating is old but consistent, and the norms derived from it are specific. Google's 2009 web-log study put the share of users on the newest minor version within 21 days at **Chrome 97%, Firefox 85%, Safari 53%, Opera 24%**, concluding that "silent updates and little dependency on the underlying operating system are most effective" (co-authored by Google; 17 years old) ([Full Disclosure, May 2009](https://seclists.org/fulldisclosure/2009/May/39); [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/risk/google-chrome-update-scheme-beats-firefox-safari-opera); [Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/google-chrome-trumps-browser-pack-in-update-test/)). SOUPS 2014 found "28 out of our 37 participants had a misunderstanding about what was happening on their computer" under silent Windows updating and that "not all updates are wanted" ([Wash et al.](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2014/proceedings/presentation/wash)); SOUPS 2016 found "updates interrupt users, users lack sufficient information to decide whether or not to update, and vary in terms of how they want to be notified and provide consent" ([Mathur et al.](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2016/technical-sessions/presentation/mathur); [UMD HCIL](https://www.umiacs.umd.edu/news-events/news/hcil-researchers-present-paper-soups-2016); its four recommendations could not be extracted); USEC 2019 examined forced Windows 10 Home updates and perceived control ([Morris et al.](https://sec.cs.ucl.ac.uk/fileadmin/sec/publications/morris-2019-control.pdf), body not retrievable). Vendor practice converges on download silently, install at quit or next launch, nag for about a week, then force: Chrome updates "in the background when you close and reopen", its enterprise `RelaunchNotificationPeriod` defaults to seven days with relaunches aimed at 2–4 am and `Required` forcing at the end ([Update Google Chrome](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/95414); [Notify users to relaunch](https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/7679871); [Chromium policy](https://developer.samsung.com/browser/policy/relaunch-notification.html); [Edge policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ie/deployedge/microsoft-edge-browser-policies/relaunchnotificationperiod)); Firefox's background update agent runs every seven hours but is Windows-only, "macOS support is a future goal" ([Firefox: Background Updates](https://firefox-source-docs.mozilla.org/toolkit/mozapps/update/docs/BackgroundUpdates.html)); Apple's Background Security Improvements (macOS 26.1, first public release 26.3.1 (a) in January 2026) apply patches "silently in the background" with a user toggle ([heise](https://heise.de/-11069876); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/01/08/ios-263-and-macos-tahoe-263-betas-get-second-background-security-updates); [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4084124/what-are-background-security-improvements-on-apple-devices.html); [mjtsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=49341)); the Mac App Store's factory default for automatic updates is not stated on Apple's page ([Change App Store settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/app-store/mchlp2257/mac)); Microsoft's Windows deadlines (doc dated 30 June 2026) suggest 0–1 days for quality updates, 2 days for feature updates plus a 2-day grace, with forced restart after the deadline and a 15-minute warning ([Enforce compliance deadlines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/deployment/update/wufb-compliancedeadlines)). Staged rollouts: the App Store's phased release delivers 1%, 2%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 50%, 100% over seven days to users with automatic updates on, "without any notification", pausable for 30 days total, while anyone can still download manually ([App Store Connect: phased release](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/update-your-app/release-a-version-update-in-phases/)); Google Play's percentage "won't increase automatically", halting means "no additional users will receive the app version" while existing recipients "remain on that version", and a fix is a new release ([Google Play: staged rollouts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6346149)); Mozilla enabled Firefox updates "at a rate of 25%", waited a day, then dropped to 0% to inspect crashes, with Balrog's `backgroundRate` throttling background checks only — and a 2016 bug served ~100% at a nominal 0% ([hearsum.ca, Feb 2016](https://hearsum.ca/posts/streamlining-throttled-rollout-of-firefox-releases/); [Balrog](https://mozilla-balrog.readthedocs.io/en/latest/database.html); [Bugzilla 1282838](https://bugzil.la/1282838)). Rollback is server-side or by shipping a higher-numbered build: Squirrel.Mac's server can serve an older version, Tauri downgrades only with a custom comparator, electron-updater needs `allowDowngrade`, Chrome allows `RollbackToTargetVersion` to the three latest majors, Google Play has no rollback, and Sparkle neither retains the previous bundle nor documents rollback ([Squirrel.Mac](https://github.com/Squirrel/Squirrel.Mac); [Tauri 2](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/updater/); [electron-builder](https://www.electron.build/docs/features/auto-update); [Manage Chrome updates](https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/7591084); [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6346149); [Sparkle delta updates](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/delta-updates/)). For a vendor with 1–20 Macs per client, percentage phasing is meaningless inside one firm; the useful analogue is Firefox's "25% for a day" applied across clients — one canary firm or one canary Mac per firm, a day, then all — plus a halt switch in the index, with Google Play's "halt stops new installs, nobody is rolled back" as the honest model of what a static channel can do, and "rollback" meaning a higher-numbered build of the old code, which is only safe if data-format migrations stay backward-compatible for at least one version (inferred).

### Local data survives upgrades only with a version in the file, a copy before migration, a refusal to open newer data, and a schema that only grows

Shipping local-first products converge on the same kit, and SQLite supplies the primitives. `PRAGMA user_version` "is an integer that is available to applications to use however they want" at header offset 60, `PRAGMA application_id` at offset 68 is "a unique integer so that utilities such as file(1) can determine the specific file type", and SQLite's own `schema_version` must not be touched ("Misuse of this pragma can result in database corruption"); `PRAGMA integrity_check` returns a single `ok` row or up to 100 errors and "does not find FOREIGN KEY errors", with `quick_check` as the O(N) variant ([SQLite pragma docs](https://www.sqlite.org/pragma.html)). Copies must be taken through SQLite, not `cp`: "Systems that run automatic backups in the background might try to make a backup copy of an SQLite database file while it is in the middle of a transaction. The backup copy then might contain some old and some new content, and thus be corrupt"; `sqlite3_rsync`, the backup API or `VACUUM INTO` "will work even on a live database", a plain copy is safe only with no transaction in progress and only if the `-wal`/`-journal` files travel too, network filesystems "and NFS in particular" corrupt through buggy locks, and "unlinking or renaming an open database file results in behavior that is undefined" ([How To Corrupt An SQLite Database File](https://www.sqlite.org/howtocorrupt.html); [Backup API](https://www.sqlite.org/backup.html); [VACUUM INTO](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_vacuum.html), shipped in 3.27.0 on 7 February 2019 per the [release log](https://www.sqlite.org/releaselog/3_27_0.html)). Downgrade refusal is standard: Firefox 67 (21 May 2019) "will now protect you against running older versions of the browser" because of data corruption, each installation gets its own profile, and the check reads `compatibility.ini` with an `-allow-downgrade` escape documented only in community threads ([Firefox 67.0 notes](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/firefox/67.0/releasenotes/); [Dedicated profiles](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/dedicated-profiles-firefox-installation); [Install an older version](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/install-older-version-of-firefox); [forum](https://support.mozilla.org/ar/questions/1267878); [Forsyth Tech](https://its.forsythtech.edu/support/solutions/articles/10000049967); [Discourse](https://discourse.mozilla.org/t/firefox-user-experience/75252)); Lightroom Classic names the catalog with its format (`-v12.lrcat`), says "a copy is created before the upgrade and stored as a compressed file within the catalog folder", and refuses a catalog "too new to be used by this version" ([Catalog FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/technical-support/workflow-issues/catalog-issues/catalog-faq-lightroom.html); [Create catalogs](https://helpx.adobe.com/il_en/lightroom-classic/desktop/manage-catalogs-and-files/create-catalogs.html); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-discussions/the-lightroom-catalog-cannot-be-opened-because-it-is-too-new-to-be-used-by-this-version/m-p/15000724/highlight/true); [cannot read sync database](https://support.omnigroup.com/of-cannot-read-sync); [compatibility matrix](https://support.omnigroup.com/omnifocus-backwards-compatibility/)); Home Assistant's `Store` writes a `{version, minor_version, key, data}` envelope, calls `_async_migrate_func` only when versions differ, and raises `UnsupportedStorageVersionError` when stored `version` exceeds `max_readable_version`, with config entries gaining `MINOR_VERSION` on 18 December 2023 so that "a minor bump is safe for downgrades" and a major bump "makes the integration fail setup if the user downgrades" ([storage.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/core/dev/homeassistant/helpers/storage.py); [util/file.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/core/dev/homeassistant/util/file.py); [HA developer blog](https://developers.home-assistant.io/blog/2023/12/18/config-entry-minor-version/); [config entries](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/config_entries_index); [PrusaLink example](https://git.sudo.is/home-assistant/core/src/branch/dev/homeassistant/components/prusalink/__init__.py)); Anki backups from 2.1.50 "can't be imported into older Anki versions" and Immich says "Downgrading to an earlier version, even within the same minor version, is not supported" ([Anki backups](https://docs.ankiweb.net/backups.html); [Immich upgrading](https://docs.immich.app/install/upgrading); [Immich backup](https://docs.immich.app/administration/backup-and-restore)).

Backup-before-upgrade is contested enough that Home Assistant reversed itself within a release. 2025.1 (3 January 2025) rebuilt backups with scheduled runs, AES-128 encryption by default, retention and off-site copies, and removed the "backup before update" toggle as redundant; users said they "relied on these backups to have the latest data, for example to roll back a version", so 2025.2 (5 February 2025) restored it — off by default, showing the last backup time, creating "a full automated backup… before the update is started" — and 2025.5 (7 May 2025) added a setting for whether the toggle starts on or off because people disagreed about the default ([HA 2025.1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/01/03/release-20251/); [HA 2025.2](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/02/05/release-20252/); [HA 2025.5](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/05/07/release-20255/)); the CLI's `ha core update --backup` exists "in case you need to downgrade", downgrade is "a partial restore of a backup", and the Supervisor names the pre-update backup after the version being left (`core_<version>`) ([HA common tasks](https://www.home-assistant.io/common-tasks/os/); [Supervisor core.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/supervisor/main/supervisor/homeassistant/core.py)). Other local-first apps show the same instinct: Anki backs up every 30 minutes by default and forces a backup before "Check Database" ([Anki backups](https://docs.ankiweb.net/backups.html)); Things keeps ten daily backups beside its database but restoring is manual file renaming ([Things: backing up](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803570/); [Things: restoring](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803595/)); Lightroom tests integrity before backing up and at launch ([Adobe: Back up a catalog](https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/lightroom-classic/desktop/manage-catalogs-and-files/back-catalog.html)); Immich dumps its database daily keeping 14; Obsidian's File Recovery keeps snapshots "a minimum of 5 minutes… apart" for seven days outside the vault and tells users sync "is not designed for backups" ([Obsidian: File recovery](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/file-recovery); [Obsidian: Back up](https://obsidian.md/help/backup)); Firefox 146 (December 2025) added a built-in daily profile backup, Windows-first ([Firefox 146 notes](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/firefox/146.0/releasenotes/); [enterprise notes](https://firefox-admin-docs.mozilla.org/release-notes/version/firefox-146/); [manual backup](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/back-and-restore-information-firefox-profiles)). Integrity-check-then-restore exists in Firefox (a corrupt `places.sqlite` is renamed `.corrupt` and bookmarks restored from JSON backups, with reports of the newest backup itself being damaged) and in Home Assistant's post-update health check, which on failure logs "HomeAssistant update failed -> rollback!", files an `UPDATE_ROLLBACK` repair issue, saves `home-assistant-rollback.log` for the user and reinstalls the previous image; HA OS boots the other RAUC slot after a failed boot ([Mozilla support](https://support.mozilla.org/eu/questions/1392098); [Mozilla support](https://support.mozilla.org/af/questions/930353); [Supervisor core.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/supervisor/main/supervisor/homeassistant/core.py); [HA OS architecture](https://contributors.home-assistant.io/os/operating-system/); [HA community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/ha-os-server-crash-after-version-15-update/865029); [restore issue](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/restore-backup-not-reverting-ha-version/1009956); the "three failed boots" count only in a [German forum](https://www.computerbase.de/forum/threads/home-assistent-kein-zugriff-mehr-nach-download-backup-datei.2235508/)).

"A schema only grows" is the rule wherever user-visible data is at stake, with removal deferred to a separate deliberate step. Parallel Change (martinfowler.com, 13 May 2014) names it expand → migrate → contract ([ParallelChange](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ParallelChange.html)); strong_migrations spells out column removal as ignore → deploy → drop → deploy → un-ignore, and renames as create new, write both, backfill, move reads, drop old ([strong_migrations](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations)); Fivetran keeps a deleted source column and "write[s] NULL values into the column" from then on, dropping only on a type change or a deliberate "Drop blocked columns" ([Fivetran: column deleted](https://fivetran.com/docs/connectors/troubleshooting/column-deleted); [why deleted columns still appear](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/troubleshooting/column-deleted-from-source-is-in-destination); [Fivetran blog](https://www.fivetran.com/blog/schema-changes)); Airbyte offers four schema-change policies, never drops a removed column, and pauses the connection on a removed primary key or cursor regardless of policy, checking every 15 minutes on Cloud ([Airbyte: schema change management](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/using-airbyte/schema-change-management)); Salesforce keeps a deleted custom field restorable for **15 days** and, in managed packages, a removed field "is marked as deprecated rather than hard deleted", surfaces in "Unused Components" so the subscriber can export first, and its API name "can't be reused" ([Unused Components](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg1-dev/guide/unused-package-components.html); [SalesforceBen](https://www.salesforceben.com/how-to-restore-deleted-salesforce-fields/); [Delete a Custom Field](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=000003629&language=en_US&type=1); [2GP removal](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pkg2-dev/guide/sfdx-dev-dev2gp-remove-md-components.html); [1GP removal](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.pkg1_dev.meta/pkg1_dev/packaging_managed_component_deletion.htm); [known issue](https://help.salesforce.com/s/issue?id=a028c00000qQ9XJAA0&language=en_US)); protobuf says "Changing field numbers for any existing field is not safe", removed numbers must be `reserved`, and the wire format "doesn't provide a way to detect fields encoded using one definition and decoded using another" ([proto3 guide](https://protobuf.dev/programming-guides/proto3/)); WordPress's `dbDelta()` only ever adds or changes columns and indexes — "the old column is not removed!" ([dbDelta](https://developer.wordpress.org/reference/functions/dbdelta/)); CloudKit production schemas are literally append-only: "You can add fields to a record after production deployment, but you cannot delete a field or change its data type", because the server "must always understand record types and fields that older versions of your app might still use" ([Designing for CloudKit](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/General/Conceptual/iCloudDesignGuide/DesigningforCloudKit/DesigningforCloudKit.html); [CKRecord](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/cloudkit/ckrecord); [WWDC22 10120](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10120/)). For modules whose settings schema changes while user data exists, the shipping pattern is a defaults stack where an update changes only the defaults layer: Obsidian plugins do `Object.assign({}, DEFAULT_SETTINGS, await this.loadData())` ([Obsidian: Settings](https://docs.obsidian.md/Plugins/User+interface/Settings)); Anki add-ons keep vendor `config.json` and user `meta.json`, read meta.json "preferentially", and a changed vendor default does not reach customised configs unless the user restores defaults ([Anki add-on config](https://docs.ankiweb.net/addons/addon-config)); VS Code's scopes go Default → User → Workspace → Policy with "later scopes override earlier scopes" ([VS Code settings](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/configure/settings)); git reads system → global → local → worktree → command "with last value found taking precedence" ([git-config](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-config)); WordPress plugin deactivation "does not remove options or tables" — only `uninstall.php` does ([Uninstall methods](https://developer.wordpress.org/plugins/plugin-basics/uninstall-methods/)). On-disk separation follows platform convention: XDG's data/config/state/cache homes (state added in v0.8, 8 May 2021, for "actions history… current state of the application that can be reused on a restart") ([XDG basedir spec](https://specifications.freedesktop.org/basedir/latest/); [v0.8](https://specifications.freedesktop.org/basedir/0.8); [Guix bug](https://issues.guix.gnu.org/61809)), Apple's `~/Library/Application Support/<bundle id>/` for "files that your app creates and manages on behalf of the user and can include files that contain user data", `~/Library/Caches` that "Apps should never rely on", and never writing `~/Library/Preferences` directly ([macOS Library Directory Details, archived](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/FileManagement/Conceptual/FileSystemProgrammingGuide/MacOSXDirectories/MacOSXDirectories.html); [CFPreferences domains](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/CoreFoundation/Conceptual/CFPreferences/Concepts/PreferenceDomains.html)), Docker's "the image can be replaced; the volume persists" ([Docker: Volumes](https://docs.docker.com/engine/storage/volumes/)), Obsidian's vault folder versus system-folder global settings ([Obsidian data storage](https://obsidian.md/help/data-storage)) and Things' group-container database ([Things release notes](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/1100684/)). The conservative reading for a vendor that cannot see the data: resolve settings as default < vendor-managed < user, write only to the user layer, and never apply a changed vendor default over a value the person customised (inferred).

Reading outlook.office.com in the person's own browser is not a recognised posture: the Online Services Acceptable Use Policy (last updated February 2011) forbids using "any automated process or service to access or use the Services" "such as a BOT, a spider or periodic caching", a licensing analyst reports the 15 June 2026 Product Terms added "to scrape or use other data extraction methods to extract data from the Online Service" across all Online Services (not verified against Microsoft's text), and the consumer Services Agreement (effective 30 September 2026) bars "impermissible scraping" ([AUP](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/legal/docid12); [SAMexpert, July 2026](https://samexpert.com/microsoft-product-terms-july-2026/); [Product Terms](https://www.microsoft.com/licensing/terms/product/ForOnlineServices/all); [Services Agreement](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/servicesagreement)). Dust's Outlook connector is the worked example of the sanctioned route: "Each user authenticates with their own Microsoft account", an Entra admin grants consent for the organisation, delegated scopes are `Mail.ReadWrite`, `Mail.Send`, `User.Read`, `SensitivityLabel.Read`, `offline_access`, and "There is no read-only Outlook Mail setup" (early access 30 July 2025) ([Dust: Microsoft Outlook](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/user-documentation/agents/tools/microsoft-outlook.md); [Dust changelog](https://docs.dust.tt/changelog/connect-and-manage-outlook-emails-in-dust)); Copilot Studio's "User authentication" shows a connection card, the user consents in a separate window, and "Copilot Studio doesn't store any credentials" ([Copilot Studio user authentication](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/configure-enduser-authentication)).

Gmail is the expensive one. Every read scope except add-on scopes is *restricted* — `gmail.readonly`, `gmail.modify`, `gmail.metadata`, `gmail.compose`, `gmail.insert`, `gmail.settings.*`, `https://mail.google.com/` — `gmail.send` is sensitive and only `gmail.labels` is non-sensitive, and "If you store restricted scope data on servers (or transmit), then you must go through a security assessment" ([Gmail API scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes)). An unverified app is "limited to 100 new users until it is verified", a cap that "is permanent for the project's lifetime"; apps in Testing are "limited to up to 100 test users" whose "Authorizations… will expire seven days from the time of consent" unless only basic profile scopes are requested ([Unverified apps](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/7454865); [Manage app audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)). Restricted-scope verification needs the narrowest scopes, a demo video of the OAuth flow, Limited Use compliance and "an annual security assessment from a Google empanelled group of security assessors", re-verified annually ([Restricted scope verification](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321); [OAuth verification overview](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/9110914)); Google assigns CASA AL1 or AL2, "the Google Trust and Safety team will contact you", a pass yields a Letter of Validation, and the ADA's tiering page (27 June 2026) describes both levels as lab-tested with no self-scan tier ([Security assessment](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13465431); [CASA tiering](https://appdefensealliance.dev/casa/casa-tiering); [CASA](https://appdefensealliance.dev/casa)). Google publishes no price; Leviathan lists AL1 at "$3,000 for No Rush, $4,500 for Standard, and $6,000 for Priority" over "2-4 weeks", a 2024 comparison cites TAC Security at "$540 per app", and vendors selling alternatives quote "$540–$1k/yr… up to $15k-$75k on the legacy track" and "$15,000 to $75,000" (all third-party, conflicting, interested) ([Leviathan](https://www.leviathansecurity.com/programs/google-casa-cloud-application-security-assessment); [switchlabs](https://www.switchlabs.dev/post/casa-tier-2-tier-3-security-review-providers-pricing-and-the-cheapest-option); [Unipile](https://www.unipile.com/integrating-google-oauth-2-0-user-authentication-into-your-app/); [Truto](https://truto.one/google/); Nylas warns `gmail.modify` for read-only use triggers a heavier tier at [Nylas](https://developer.nylas.com/docs/cookbook/use-cases/build/google-oauth-scopes/)). The exemption that matters for a client firm: "Internal Use apps" — "The project must be owned by the organization, and its OAuth Consent Screen must be configured for internal use", which removes the unverified screen and the 100-user cap; and a Workspace admin "can add a third-party app to the list of trusted apps in the Google Admin console" ([When verification is not needed](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464323)). Glean offers exactly this choice — "Central (recommended): Glean's managed OAuth app" or "Custom: your own Google OAuth app" — with "Each teammate authorizes their own Google account over OAuth the first time they use a tool" ([Glean Gmail setup](https://docs.glean.com/connectors/native/gmail/new-setup)). So a vendor-published external Gmail app that reads mail cannot avoid verification plus annual CASA and the interim 100-user cap, while a per-firm Internal project avoids both at the cost of a Google Cloud project per firm and a Workspace admin's involvement; the scopes page ties the assessment to storing data "on servers", which a local-first app can argue but not rely on, since Google's assessment page says restricted-scope apps "must undergo" it (inferred). Reading Gmail in the person's signed-in browser appears in no Google developer document; the Terms of Service (30 July 2026) target "using automated means to access content from any of our services in violation of the machine-readable instructions" and "bypassing our systems or protective measures", so it is a grey area with no published safe harbour ([Google Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms)).

LinkedIn offers no sanctioned path for what automation vendors sell, and its enforcement in 2025–2026 hit vendors' own presence. ### Conclusion

Read together, the six bodies of evidence describe one architecture rather than six policies. The app bundle is replaced wholesale and signed by Apple; vendor-shipped connectors and kits are versioned directories listed in a signed, expiring, serial-numbered index with per-file hashes, installed into a vendor-owned folder the updater may rewrite; and the person's world file, connections and customised copies live in a third home the updater never touches, with a recorded baseline for every vendor thing the person has altered so that an update can tell "vendor changed it" from "both changed it" and leave a visible marker instead of a merge. The silent-versus-ask line is then mechanical: a release that needs no new input, scope or data-format change from the person is applied at launch with a notice; anything else becomes a dated ask with a stated end-state, and nothing the person touched is ever deleted by an update — it is deprecated, side-filed, or left for the person to remove. What is genuinely new in 2026 is how little the agent ecosystems have adopted of this: the skill format has no version field, the plugin systems do not sign, auto-update is off by default, and the one registry that skipped review was poisoned within weeks of launch, so a small vendor shipping connector directories has to supply the signing, expiry and pinning discipline itself rather than inherit it.

The compliance findings shift the design more than the engineering ones. Every sanctioned route — Claude, Outlook, Gmail — reduces to the same shape the integration platforms use: the vendor maintains the know-how, the person authenticates with their own credential through the provider's own flow, and the credential is bound to the person's install, never to the artifact; the differences are only in who must sign a form (an Entra admin, a Workspace admin, an Anthropic salesperson) and what it costs (nothing, a Partner Program account, or an annual CASA lab fee). The two places where the notes found no safe harbour — reading outlook.office.com or Gmail inside the person's own browser, and any LinkedIn reading at all — are also the two places where the shipping products either stayed inside the official API's narrow actions or pushed the risk onto a disclaiming intermediary. A founder deciding today should treat "browser reading of mailboxes" as a stopgap with a migration plan to Graph and to per-firm Internal Gmail projects, treat LinkedIn as a per-person, person-initiated, human-paced capability whose failure mode is the person's account rather than the product, and treat Anthropic's "mutually agreed otherwise" as a conversation to open before any vendor-paid key ships, not after. The open gaps that most affect these decisions are whether Anthropic will publish approval criteria, whether Google's CASA scoping accepts a local-only data argument, and whether Microsoft's June 2026 scraping clause reads as the analyst reports — each is checkable against a primary page that the notes could not fetch.
