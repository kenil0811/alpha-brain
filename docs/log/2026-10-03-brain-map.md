# The map of the brain (3 October 2026, late night; Q30)

Kenil, on the map of work: "when and how does it refresh?" and "the graph should be meaningful
and maybe generated via AI; I don't just want random nodes; let's discuss first." The
discussion, in short: the map of work was true but it was plumbing; what he wants to see is
"what my brain is, what's concentrated, what's important, what's not linked"; AI-inferred
links are worth a try if each shows its reason and can be corrected; keep the work map as a
toggle; refresh only on demand. Those four answers are Q30.

**The map of the brain** (`context/graph.py`, `world_graph`; `GET /api/graph?kind=world`).
The person at the centre, with what Alpha knows about them; their areas (modules) weighted by
what happened in each in the last 30 days (the journal, counted); the tables, documents, pages
and goals in each area; the people and organisations Alpha knows, linked to the rows that are
them, the documents about them and the areas that named them; and the links Alpha proposed or
the person accepted. Nothing is stored for the map; it is computed on each ask.

**Alpha's links** (`runtime/connecting.py`, `POST /api/graph/connect`, run by Refresh). A cheap
no-tools run of the small model, as noticing uses, sees the things on the map, the links
already known (an area counts), and the evidence around them: the recent journal lines that
named something, the pages' openings, the documents' first lines. It proposes links as JSON,
each with a reason and the line of evidence that says so. A proposal is kept only when both
ends are on the map, one of them is a person, an organisation or a document (the things a fact
can be about; a document through its entity), the two are not already linked, and the source
names something in the evidence; what is dropped, and why, goes to the log. Each kept link is
a suggested fact (`related_to`, with `why` and `source`) that the map draws dashed, with
"Alpha thinks: …" and Yes/No in the card (the existing fact decision); a yes makes it a plain
link. Alpha thinks, because; never Alpha knows.

**The window** (`shell/map/`). A World/Work toggle; the brain's kinds with their own glyphs and
colours (you, people, organisations, documents, pages, goals, areas, tables); people,
organisations and documents that nothing connects wear a dotted ring and the glance in the
corner counts them ("Not linked: 20 things nothing connects", a click lights them), names the
areas with most going on and the quietest, and how many of Alpha's links wait for a yes. The
map asks the core only when a view is first opened and on Refresh, never on a clock (the half-
minute refresh of the first pass is gone); it says "as of 19:12"; a refresh keeps every node's
place. Refresh on the World view runs Alpha's link-finding first ("Alpha is looking for
links…"), then redraws.

**What ran, on a fresh backup copy of Kenil's world.** The brain map: 47 things (13 people, 13
tables, 7 documents, 6 areas, 6 pages, 1 goal, and Kenil), all 20 people and documents not
linked, Advisory (196), Deal Tracker (172) and Network (58) the busiest areas, Notes the
quietest. The first run of the link-finding pass proposed eleven links and kept none: every one
was between an area and a document or a table, which cannot carry a fact, and most repeated a
document's own area, which the model had not been told. So the rules changed: the evidence now
lists the areas as known links; a document may carry a fact through its entity; a proposal that
repeats a known link is dropped; the model is asked for the links a person would want (which
person a document is about, which area a person belongs with, who shares an organisation). The
second run (42 s) kept one: the "ETA Tracker Accounting Vikas.csv" from the watched folder
belongs with Deal Tracker, because "the CSV lists accounting practice broker sites from ETA
Tracker platforms", with the Deal Tracker page's opening as its source. The thirteen LinkedIn
people got nothing, rightly: nothing in the evidence connects them to anything yet. Tests: three
more in the core (the brain map holds what the world holds; connecting keeps the grounded links
and drops the rest, with the log saying why; a bad reply proposes nothing), three more in the
desktop (not-linked rings and the glance; a proposed link's reason and a yes; the core asked
only on first open and on Refresh, Refresh running the pass); 193 core and 65 desktop tests,
lint and types clean.

**What it will take to be the map Kenil wants.** The map is honest today: it shows a brain
with six busy areas and twenty things nothing connects. The links worth having come from
three sources still pending: readers declaring their rows as people (the 1,551 connections
become people with their companies); noticing over what Alpha reads, so an email or a document
yields who it is from and about; and Alpha's link-finding, which grows with that evidence. The
first is pending item 6.
