# The map of Alpha's work (3 October 2026, night; the graph proposal's option C)

Kenil chose option C of `docs/design/knowledge-graph-proposal.md`: a map of Alpha's own work,
now, as a fifth tab of Intelligence; the graph of the world (people, documents, pages) waits
for people-for-real. "Make the graph look good and interactive and fun."

**The core** (`api/graph.py`, `GET /api/graph?kind=work`). Nodes are what the Intelligence
tabs list: modules, tables, skills (read, act, run), automations, the sources a module reads
from, and connections (a signed-in site, a watched folder). Edges are the world's own kinds of
relation, each with its source in the world: a table is *in* a module; an automation *runs* a
run skill; a run skill *runs* its readers in order (step n) and *tells* a table; a reader
*reads into* a table (said by step n of the run skill); a source is *read by* its reader; a
sign-in is *signed in at* every skill on a site it covers; a folder *feeds* a module with the
documents it gave. A skill carries its runs and failures in the last 30 days from the journal
(the entries naming a reader, a procedure or a skill), its health and last problem; a source
its status (working, needs a sign-in, blocked, unavailable, not read yet, skipped); an
automation on, off or a last run failed. Nothing is stored: the map is computed on each ask.
`kind=world` is refused plainly until it exists. On Kenil's world: 89 nodes, 131 edges.

**The window** (`shell/map/`). `layout.ts` is a d3-force simulation (one new dependency,
`d3-force`): modules are hubs their members are pulled to, run skills sit between their
automation and their readers, readers by the table they feed; bigger nodes for modules and for
what holds or runs the most. `layout.worker.ts` runs it off the main thread and streams
positions every other tick, takes a dragged node (held in place, the rest make room), its
release, and a shake; without a worker (a test, or a webview that will not start one) the
layout settles at once on the main thread, the same picture without the motion. `WorkMap.tsx`
draws it in SVG with pan (drag the background), zoom (the wheel, about the cursor), Fit, Shake,
a legend whose chips hide a kind, hover (the neighbourhood lights, the rest dims), a click
(a card: what it is, its state in words, runs and failures, every link in words, Open), a
double-click (its page). Modules, tables, automations and connections keep their names on
the map; skills and sources show theirs up close or when lit, so eighty-nine things stay
readable; labels shrink less than the map does. A broken skill or a blocked source pulses red,
a thing switched off is faded. The window gives nodes their addresses (a skill opens its page,
an automation its page, a table or a source its module, a connection the Connections tab).

**What ran.** In the browser pane against a check core on a fresh backup copy of Kenil's
world, at 1240×820: the map settled and fitted itself in about two seconds; Deal Tracker's
wheel of 15 sources and 14 readers around Deal Listings, Advisory, Job Search, Network,
Nutrition and Notes as their own clusters; hovering Deal Tracker lit its neighbourhood;
clicking LinkedIn connections opened its card ("working · 9 runs in 30 days, 4 failed",
"read by" its source, "linkedin.com signed in at this") and Open went to the skill's page;
Shake re-settled the layout. No console errors. Tests: two in the core (every node and edge
kind on a built world, with their sources; an empty world an empty map), four in the desktop
(the layout settles finitely with members near their module; the map draws, the card opens
and opens the page; a chip hides a kind; addresses and words); 190 core and 57 desktop tests,
typecheck and lint clean.
