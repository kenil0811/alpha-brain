# Modules as workflows (11 October 2026, Q39): the decision, no code changed

After M1 of the car wash program (7,710 parcels, a 33-column table, a pass list resting on
borrowed interstate counts, a job form bolted on so the person could do anything at all),
Kenil stopped it: "We need to take a step back and re think about this thoroughly. Such a
complicated output is not useful / intuitive for a user at all." The rethink, slowly, by
questions and answers (his, in order): the user's procedure comes first (a process document
and a scorecard exist in `project-waterways-main-2/inputs/`); the output they want is a score
and a memo to investors; a short interview in chat with decisive questions and safe defaults,
guidelines not hard limits; suggestions with the first version, sources kept and shown only on
ask; a list, a detailed page, a memo and notes, a map nice to have; sites typed first, read
from email later; nothing outward without asking, reads at Alpha's judgement; every setting
and assumption visible and editable; done means it replaces the manual flow; one module per
workflow, built from replaceable parts, extensible without redoing; normalized tables, not one
wide one, and documents that are not tables. Then: the understanding as a page the person
changes through chat, not by hand; data preserved on change where possible, never the person's
own entries; one person for now; documents kept, exportable, usable from other modules; one
whole first version, thin but never garbage; a library of parts shared across modules now and
people later. And two corrections of mine: no fixed kinds of step ("we are not hardcoding
anything"), and modules stay flexible ("if the user at some point asks that they want a map,
alpha should do it").

The concept is §12 of the design, with the car wash as the person would see it; Q39 records
the decision; §6.3 and §6.4 keep their mechanisms and lose their shape. No code changed. The
first build and its acceptance are STATE's pending item 9.
